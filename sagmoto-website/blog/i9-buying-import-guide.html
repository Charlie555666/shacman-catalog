SAGMOTO i9 Buying and Import Guide for European Fleet Operators

August 28, 2026Buying and Import Guide12 min read

European fleet operators are buying the SAGMOTO i9 4x2 electric rigid truck in increasing numbers as EU zero-emission zone mandates converge across most major capitals in 2026. The i9 is one of the few Chinese-manufactured electric trucks in the 18-tonne GVW class with EU whole-vehicle type approval (WVTA), CCS2 DC fast charging compatibility, and a published European aftersales network. This guide walks through every decision point in specifying, financing, importing, bodying, registering, and operating the i9 in Europe, with specific reference to the German, Dutch, French, Spanish, and Italian market conditions.

Step 1: Define the Duty Cycle and ZEV Eligibility

The i9 is the right platform only if the duty cycle is consistent with urban and regional distribution under the EU ZEV restrictions that apply to the operating corridors. European ZEV mandates that drive i9 adoption in 2026 include the German LEZ Umwelt-Zone (with LKW-only access from July 2027 onward), the Netherlands zero-emission zones in 25+ municipalities (effective 2025-2030), Paris Zone à Faibles Émissions (ZFEm, currently in force for trucks), Madrid Madrid Central restriction (extending to trucks in 2026), Italian area C Milan, ZTL Rome, ZES Bari, and Rotterdam, Amsterdam, and Utrecht ZEV access. For each corridor where the i9 will operate, the operator must verify the local ZEV access criteria (typically weight-class-based or CO2-class-based) and the calendar for full ZEV-only mandate application.

For fleet operators running urban delivery with a 200-280 km daily radius, depot overnight charging, and a structured 5-shift-week schedule, the i9 is well-matched. For operators running extended regional routes beyond 300 km daily radius, motorway corridor duty, or off-depot charging events, the i9 should be supplemented by diesel tractors or by higher-capacity EV platforms.

Step 2: Choose the i9 Specification

The i9 is offered in two battery sizes (246 kWh and 282 kWh) and three cab variants (H4R narrow day cab for urban ULEZ access, H6 standard day cab, and H9C sleeper cab for overnight airport return). The 246 kWh variant is sufficient for the typical urban fleet duty cycle, but the 282 kWh variant is the more practical choice for two-shift operation or for fleets running 12-14 hour daily duty cycles that include motorway segments.

The i9 chassis wheelbase options are 4,200 mm (single-axle rigid, 6.0-7.2 m body), 4,800 mm (single-axle rigid, 7.2-8.4 m body), and 5,800 mm (single-axle rigid, 8.4-9.2 m body or 20-ft container chassis). For German 18-tonne ZEV distribution, the 4,800 mm wheelbase is the most flexible configuration. For narrow-body Dutch delivery work, the 4,200 mm wheelbase is the right choice. For overnight refrigerated container return, the 5,800 mm wheelbase with 20-ft chassis is the standard.

Specification guide: Specify the 246 kWh battery for urban-only duty; specify the 282 kWh battery for two-shift or motorway-corridor duty. Specify the H4R narrow cab for ULEZ-restricted cities (London, Munich, Rotterdam). Specify the H9C sleeper for overnight duty (Paris-Florence, Brussels-Geneva, Hamburg-Zurich corridor).

Step 3: Bodywork and Refrigeration Integration

i9 chassis-cab units are bodied in Europe through four certified European bodybuilders: Evo Cold (Germany/Netherlands), Lamberet (France), Lecuyer (UK/Ireland), and MBB Palfinger (Italy/Spain). For dry freight box body, payload spans 9.2-10.5 tonne depending on body width (2,460 mm Euro-1 standard or 2,500 mm wide) and liftgate inclusion (1,500 kg tuck-away or 2,500 kg column lift). For refrigerated body with Thermal King, Carrier, or Zanotti refrigeration unit, refrigeration system capacity is sized to the duty cycle (typically T-Series V-Series for ZEV duty, with electric standby driven by the i9 high-voltage system for off-road load pre-cooling during depop runs).

For chassis-driven conversions (refrigerated distribution, container swap-body, refuse collection, or mobile workshop), the i9 supports the 24V EDS electrical interface for bodybuilder integration, and the high-voltage 640V interface for directly-driven electric refrigeration or pumps. Body-to-chassis CAD drawings and electrical schematics are issued to the certified bodybuilder within 14 days of order confirmation. Lead time from order to bodied delivery varies between 9 and 14 weeks depending on the body type.

Step 4: EU Type Approval and Homologation Status

The SAGMOTO i9 is sold in two homologation states: WVTA-approved cab-chassis configurations and non-approved configurations that require individual national homologation per vehicle. The WVTA configuration is suitable for fleets importing across multiple EU countries and is required for individual registration in any EU member state without additional testing. The non-approved configuration is suitable for fleets importing to a single country where a Type-II national homologation process can be managed (typically Germany, Netherlands, Italy, France, Spain, and the Nordic countries operate Type-II homologation routes).

For WVTA-approved configurations, the homologation cost is included in the SAGMOTO pre-delivery price (typically EUR 8,500 to EUR 12,000 per chassis-cab configuration, single payment per model variant). For non-approved configurations, the individual homologation cost ranges between EUR 6,500 and EUR 11,000 per vehicle depending on the country of registration and the body type. The homologation timeline for WVTA configurations is 6-9 weeks; for individual homologation, 12-18 weeks.

Step 5: Pricing, Payment Terms, and Financing

The 2026 indicative FOB Shanghai pricing for the SAGMOTO i9 chassis-cab is between USD 95,500 and USD 124,500 depending on battery size, cab width, and equipment level. CIF European port pricing (Rotterdam, Hamburg, Antwerp, Marseille) adds 6-9 percent to FOB depending on the shipping line, container access, and EUR/USD exchange rate movement. Bodywork, country-specific homologation, and VAT add another 16-22 percent to the landed cost depending on the EU member state. The all-in landed cost for a 246 kWh H6 i9 with 7.2 m refrigerated body registered in Germany is approximately EUR 198,000-218,000.

Payment terms for i9 imports follow a standard 30 percent T/T deposit on order confirmation, 60 percent T/T against bill of lading copy after pre-shipment inspection, and 10 percent T/T after Post-Loading Survey. Letter of Credit at sight is available for orders above USD 500,000 total value and is the typical term for fleet buyers financing through German Sparkasse or Dutch ING. EU-financed operators should also evaluate BNP Paribas Asset Finance or KfW Umweltprogramm 299 financing for ZEV trucks, which offers lower interest rates than commercial fleet financing on vehicles meeting Euro VI environmental criteria.

Step 6: Aftersales and Warranty Coverage

The SAGMOTO i9 carries a 5-year / 250,000 km bumper-to-bumper warranty in Europe, a 5-year / 400,000 km or 70 percent SOH battery warranty, and a 7-year / 1,000,000 km drivetrain warranty for the central e-motor and the 640V power electronics. The aftersales service network is structured through regional agents in major capitals (Munich, Rotterdam, Paris, Madrid, Milan, Brussels, Hamburg) and through the SAGMOTO-direct technical hotline for cross-border fleet operations.

For commercial fleets with intense duty cycles, the SAGMOTO European Aftersales team offers a three-year scheduled maintenance contract covering high-voltage diagnostic checks, brake fluid exchange, cabin air filter replacement, air conditioning service, software updates, and 24-hour road assistance across the EU. The contract cost is approximately EUR 2,800 per truck per year. For fleets without the maintenance contract, SAGMOTO charges EUR 240 per diagnostic visit plus parts at dealer cost.

Step 7: Charging Infrastructure Preparation

For depot-only overnight charging, the depot electrical infrastructure is the hidden cost most often underestimated. A 250 kW DC charger requires 400A three-phase connection to the depot medium-voltage transformer; a 150 kW DC charger requires 250A three-phase. For a fleet of 5 trucks, the depot retrofit (transformer upgrade, cable trays, ground-mount chargers) typically costs EUR 35,000-65,000 depending on whether the depot owns or rents the electrical feed. For a fleet of 20 trucks, the cost scales to EUR 180,000-280,000 with dual 250 kW chargers and a ring distribution architecture.

SAGMOTO supports fleet buyers in commissioning the depot charging installation through a partner network of European EV infrastructure contractors (Allego, Vattenfall, Iberdrola in respective markets). The depot commissioning typically runs 8-14 weeks from order to operational status. Fleet buyers should plan depot retrofit 12-16 weeks ahead of vehicle delivery.

Conclusion

The SAGMOTO i9 in 246 kWh or 282 kWh specification is a viable European fleet purchase for urban and regional distribution under ZEV mandates, with a clear advantage over European competitors in acquisition cost and over BYD in fast-charging and configurability. The buying and import process is well-structured but takes 12-20 weeks from order to bodied delivery, and the depot charging infrastructure preparation runs in parallel.
