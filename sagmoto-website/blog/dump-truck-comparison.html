SAGMOTO Dump Truck Guide: X3s vs X6 vs X9 for Mining & Construction

August 4, 2026 Comparison 7 min read

Dump trucks are the backbone of mining, quarrying, and construction operations worldwide. SAGMOTO's dump truck lineup spans three distinct series — the X3s, X6, and X9 — each engineered for a different point on the payload-and-terrain spectrum. Choosing the wrong series for your operation means either paying for capability you will never use or watching an underspecified truck fail prematurely under loads it was never built to carry. This guide compares all three series across engine performance, chassis construction, payload capacity, body volume, suspension, and ideal applications, so you can match the truck to the job with confidence.

SAGMOTO X3s: The Heavy-Duty Mining Workhorse

The X3s is SAGMOTO's flagship heavy-duty dump platform, purpose-built for mining, quarry, and large-scale earthmoving operations. In its 8x4 configuration, the X3s is engineered to operate at a Gross Combination Weight (GCW) rating of up to 100 tons, placing it firmly in the heavy-mining category alongside dedicated off-highway dump trucks but with the added flexibility of on-road registration for site-to-site transfers.

Engine and Powertrain

The X3s dump truck is powered by the Cummins ISMe 420, an 8.9-liter inline-six turbocharged diesel producing 420 horsepower at 2100 RPM and 2000 Nm of peak torque between 1100 and 1700 RPM. This engine was selected specifically for mining dump applications because its torque curve delivers maximum pulling force precisely in the RPM range where a loaded dump truck climbs out of a pit or ramp. The ISMe 420 is paired with a Fast Gear 12JS240TA 12-speed manual transmission, rated to 2400 Nm, providing the torque headroom needed for repeated full-load cycles. The transmission uses a twin-countershaft design with a high/low range and splitter, giving the driver 12 forward gears to optimize engine speed for any grade.

Chassis Construction

The X3s chassis is where the heavy-duty commitment becomes visible. The frame rails use a double-layer reinforced construction — a main longitudinal rail with an inner reinforcement liner extending the full length of the load-bearing section. This double-layer design nearly doubles the frame's resistance to bending and torsional stress compared to a single-rail layout, which is critical in mining operations where trucks are loaded by large shovels and loaders that impose shock loads far exceeding the static payload rating. Cross-members are heavier-gauge than standard logistics-truck equivalents, and the frame is treated with an anti-corrosion process suited to the acidic and alkaline environments found in mine sites.

Body and Payload

The X3s mining dump body is available in capacities up to 30 cubic meters, fabricated from high-strength wear-resistant steel plate (Hardox-class equivalent) with a minimum Brinell hardness of 400 HBW. The body floor is typically specified at 8mm to 12mm thickness, with side walls at 6mm to 8mm, depending on the material density being hauled. The hydraulic lifting system uses a front-telescopic multi-stage cylinder, capable of raising a full load to discharge angle in 20 to 25 seconds.

Suspension

The X3s uses a reinforced leaf-spring suspension on both front and rear axles, with the rear tandem axle equipped with heavy-duty auxiliary springs that engage under full load. This provides a compliant ride when empty (reducing driver fatigue and chassis stress on return trips) while delivering the load-carrying stiffness needed when the truck is at full payload. For extreme off-road mining applications, an optional pneumatically suspended rear axle with load-sensing damping is available.

Ideal applications: Open-pit mining, quarry operations, large-scale construction earthmoving, ore haulage, coal transport, and any operation where payloads exceed 40 tons per trip and road conditions are unmade or severely degraded.

SAGMOTO X6: The Medium-Duty Construction All-Rounder

The X6 occupies the middle ground of the SAGMOTO dump lineup. As a 6x4 configuration with a GVWR of approximately 25 tons, it is the most versatile dump truck in the range — large enough for serious construction work, compact enough to operate on urban streets and access-restricted sites where an 8x4 cannot maneuver.

Engine Options

Unlike the X3s, which is standardized on the Cummins ISMe, the X6 offers two engine pathways to suit different market preferences and parts ecosystems:

  • Weichai WP7 / WP10: The Weichai option delivers 270 to 340 horsepower and 1100 to 1600 Nm of torque. Weichai engines are favored in markets with established Shaanxi Automobile Group service networks, offering proven durability in dusty, high-temperature environments and excellent low-end torque for stop-go construction cycles.
  • Yuchai YC6A / YC6L: The Yuchai option produces 290 to 350 horsepower with comparable torque output. Yuchai engines are preferred in markets that prioritize fuel economy and lower noise levels, and they offer an alternative parts channel independent of the Weichai network.

Both engine options are paired with a Fast Gear 9-speed or 12-speed manual transmission, scaled to the engine's torque output. The 6x4 drive layout provides the traction needed for soft ground and steep ramps typical of construction sites.

Body and Payload

The X6 dump body ranges from 18 to 22 cubic meters, fabricated from standard high-strength steel plate with wear-resistant floor options available for abrasive materials like sand, gravel, and crushed stone. The body is optimized for typical construction aggregates rather than the high-density ores the X3s is built to handle. The hydraulic system uses a mid-mount multi-stage cylinder, providing stable lifting with a discharge time of approximately 15 to 20 seconds.

Suspension

The X6 uses a parabolic leaf-spring front suspension and a progressive-rate leaf-spring rear suspension. The parabolic front springs offer a smoother ride than conventional multi-leaf springs, which matters for urban construction routes where the truck transitions between job sites on public roads. The rear suspension's progressive rate provides load-adaptive stiffness without the harsh empty-ride quality of a constant-rate heavy spring.

Ideal applications: Urban and suburban construction sites, road building, aggregate supply for concrete batch plants, demolition debris removal, infrastructure projects, and municipal works where maneuverability and moderate payload are required.

SAGMOTO X9: The Light-Duty Off-Road Specialist

The X9 is the most compact dump truck in the SAGMOTO lineup, configured as a 4x2 with a GVWR of approximately 12 to 16 tons. Despite its smaller footprint, the X9 is engineered with genuine off-road capability, making it the truck of choice for light construction, agriculture, and rural infrastructure projects where a medium-duty truck would be overkill and a standard pickup would be inadequate.

Engine

The X9 is powered by the Yuchai YC4E series, a 4.3-liter inline-four turbocharged diesel producing 170 to 220 horsepower and 600 to 850 Nm of torque. The Yuchai four-cylinder was selected for its fuel efficiency, compact dimensions, and low maintenance cost. While its output is modest compared to the X3s and X6, it is more than sufficient for the X9's payload range, and the smaller displacement translates to significantly lower fuel consumption — typically 15 to 20 percent less than a six-cylinder equivalent under comparable load conditions.

Body and Payload

The X9 dump body ranges from 10 to 14 cubic meters, built from standard steel plate. The body is sized for lighter materials — soil, sand, gravel, agricultural produce, and light demolition waste. The hydraulic lifting system is a single-acting telescopic cylinder with a discharge time of 12 to 15 seconds.

Suspension and Off-Road Capability

The X9 uses heavy-duty leaf springs front and rear, with a higher-than-standard ride height and increased suspension travel to handle rutted, uneven terrain. The 4x2 layout limits traction compared to the 6x4 and 8x4 models, but the X9 compensates with a limited-slip differential on the rear axle and an approach angle optimized for ramps and ditches. Ground clearance is substantially greater than a standard road-going lorry of comparable size.

Ideal applications: Light construction, agricultural material transport, rural road maintenance, small-scale earthmoving, landscaping supply, and operations where payload requirements are moderate and access is tight.

Recommendation by Application Type

Selecting the right dump truck is fundamentally about matching the truck's engineered duty cycle to your operation's actual demand profile. Below are recommendations for the most common application scenarios.

Mining and Quarry Operations

For open-pit mining, ore haulage, and quarry operations where payloads per trip exceed 40 tons, road conditions are unmade, and shock loading from large shovels is routine, the X3s 8x4 is the only appropriate choice. Its double-layer reinforced chassis, Hardox-class wear-resistant body, and 420HP Cummins powertrain are engineered specifically for this environment. Attempting to run an X6 or X9 in a mining cycle will result in premature frame fatigue, body cracking, and drivetrain failures within months rather than years.

Urban and Suburban Construction

For construction companies operating in and around cities — delivering aggregate to concrete plants, removing excavation spoil, supplying building materials to sites with restricted access — the X6 6x4 is the optimal balance of payload, maneuverability, and operating cost. Its 18-22 CBM body is sized for typical construction material densities, and its parabolic front suspension provides acceptable ride quality on public roads between job sites. The 6x4 configuration provides enough traction for soft ground without the fuel and tire cost penalty of the 8x4.

Light Construction and Agriculture

For landscaping, small-scale construction, agricultural material transport, and rural infrastructure projects where payloads are moderate (under 12 tons), routes are short, and access is tight, the X9 4x2 delivers the lowest operating cost per ton-kilometer in the lineup. Its fuel-efficient Yuchai four-cylinder, compact dimensions, and lighter weight make it the practical choice when a larger truck would be underutilized and uneconomical.

Mixed-Fleet Strategy

For larger operators running diverse projects — a mining contractor that also handles site infrastructure, or a construction group that occasionally takes on earthmoving contracts — a mixed fleet is often the most efficient approach. A common configuration pairs X3s trucks for primary haulage with one or two X6 trucks for lighter support work, site cleanup, and road access maintenance. This avoids burning the X3s's higher fuel and maintenance costs on tasks the X6 can handle.

Pro Tip: When specifying a dump truck, always factor in the density of the material you will actually haul, not just the volume. A 30 CBM body filled with iron ore at 2.5 t/m³ carries 75 tons — well within the X3s's rating. The same 30 CBM body filled with topsoil at 1.2 t/m³ carries only 36 tons, meaning an X6 could handle the same volume at lower cost. Match the body volume to your material density, not to a generic maximum.

Conclusion

The SAGMOTO dump truck lineup is engineered as a coherent system — the X3s, X6, and X9 each occupy a distinct payload and duty-cycle tier, and none is a compromised "in-between" model. The X3s is a genuine mining truck with the chassis, powertrain, and body construction to survive the harshest extraction environments. The X6 is a purpose-built construction dump truck that balances payload with the maneuverability and ride quality needed for urban operations. The X9 is a compact off-road-capable workhorse that brings genuine truck-class capability to light construction and agricultural applications where a larger truck would be wasteful. The right choice depends entirely on your material density, route conditions, and payload-per-trip requirements.
