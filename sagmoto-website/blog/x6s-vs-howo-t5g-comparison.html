SAGMOTO X6s vs HOWO T5G: Mid-Range Dump Truck Comparison

August 25, 2026Comparison Guide10 min read

The 8x4 dump truck segment is one of the most competitive categories in the Chinese heavy-duty truck export market, and the two platforms most frequently shortlisted by international fleet buyers are the SAGMOTO X6s and the Sinotruk HOWO T5G. Both trucks target the same duty cycle: 30-40 tonne GVW quarry and construction haulage, with reinforced frames, hub-reduction rear axles, and high-cycle hydraulic tipper systems. But the two platforms differ significantly in powertrain philosophy, frame engineering, after-sales ecosystem, and ultimately in total cost of ownership. This comparison examines both trucks side by side to help fleet buyers make an informed decision.

Platform Overview

The SAGMOTO X6s is the 8x4 heavy-duty dump truck in the Fenghan Trading range, positioned above the X3s as the premium platform for quarry and mining haulage. It uses Weichai powertrains exclusively, paired with Fast Gear transmissions and Hande or MAN rear axles. The HOWO T5G is the mid-range 8x4 dump truck in the Sinotruk range, positioned below the premium HOWO T7H and above the entry-level HOWO 3 series. It uses the Sinotruk D10 in-house engine, paired with the Sinotruk HW19710 transmission and Sinotruk in-house axles.

Powertrain Philosophy: The X6s uses the larger 11.6L Weichai WP12 engine with significantly higher torque (2,100-2,200 Nm vs 1,500-1,750 Nm), which is critical for mining haulage with steep gradients and high rolling resistance. The HOWO T5G uses the smaller 9.7L D10 engine with lower torque output but lower fuel consumption on flat terrain. The X6s is the stronger puller; the T5G is the more economical cruiser.

Frame and Chassis Strength

The frame is where the two platforms diverge most clearly. The X6s uses a 320mm double-layer frame with 8mm inner web and 6mm outer web, providing a section modulus of 1,485 cm3. The HOWO T5G uses a 280mm double-layer frame with 8mm inner web and 5mm outer web, providing a section modulus of approximately 1,150 cm3. The X6s frame is approximately 29 percent stiffer in bending and 22 percent stiffer in torsion, which translates directly to better durability on rough mining haul roads.

The tipper body is another differentiator. The X6s is fitted as standard with a Hardox 450 wear-resistant steel body (8mm floor, 6mm sides, 5mm front wall), while the HOWO T5G uses a Q345B standard structural steel body (6mm floor, 5mm sides, 4mm front wall). The Hardox 450 body on the X6s delivers approximately 2.5-3 times the wear life of the Q345B body on the T5G in abrasive quarry applications, an important consideration for buyers running high-frequency haul cycles.

Hydraulic Tipper System

Both trucks use a front-mounted telescopic hydraulic cylinder, but with different capacities. The X6s uses a 4-stage cylinder rated for 40T payload at 50 degree tip angle in 22 seconds, with a system pressure of 22 MPa (220 bar). The HOWO T5G uses a 3-stage cylinder rated for 30T payload at 50 degree tip angle in 18 seconds, with a system pressure of 20 MPa (200 bar). For operators running maximum GVW with sticky materials (wet clay, ore slurry), the X6s's higher lifting capacity and slower tip cycle is more appropriate; for general construction aggregate with frequent tipping, the T5G's faster cycle is more productive.

5-Year Total Cost of Ownership

The TCO comparison assumes a representative duty cycle of 80,000 km per year, quarry-to-construction-site haulage with 60 percent off-road operation, average fuel consumption of 42 L/100km (X6s) and 38 L/100km (T5G), and a 5-year ownership horizon. Fuel cost is assumed at USD 1.05 per litre (representative African / Latin American delivered cost).

The HOWO T5G is approximately USD 15,800 cheaper over 5 years, primarily due to lower fuel consumption. However, the comparison flips in severe-duty applications. In a quarry mining scenario at 50,000 km per year with 90 percent off-road operation and abrasive rock haulage, the X6s's Hardox body and stronger frame deliver a tipper body life of 6-8 years versus 3-4 years for the T5G. The body replacement differential (USD 4,500 every 3.5 years for T5G vs USD 1,500 every 6 years for X6s) closes the TCO gap, and the X6s's higher torque engine reduces transmission and clutch wear in low-speed heavy haulage.

After-Sales Ecosystem

The after-sales story is mixed. HOWO has a larger global dealer network, with particularly strong presence in Africa, the Middle East, and Southeast Asia. The HOWO parts supply chain is well-established and competitively priced. SAGMOTO has a smaller but rapidly growing dealer network, with strong regional presence in Central Asia, the Middle East, and Latin America, and a Dubai-based regional parts centre serving the GCC.

For buyers in established HOWO markets (Nigeria, Kenya, Saudi Arabia, Indonesia), the T5G offers easier parts sourcing and more technician familiarity. For buyers in markets where SAGMOTO has a strong regional partner (Peru, Kazakhstan, UAE), the X6s offers comparable parts availability with the advantage of a dedicated in-country service engineer for orders of 50 units or more.

Buyer Recommendations

Based on the comparison, the buyer recommendations are as follows:

  • Choose the SAGMOTO X6s if: Your duty cycle includes quarry mining or abrasive rock haulage, your routes have gradients exceeding 6 percent, you need maximum tipper body life, or you operate in a market where SAGMOTO has an established regional partner.
  • Choose the HOWO T5G if: Your duty cycle is predominantly flat-terrain construction hauling, you prioritise lowest fuel consumption, your operation is in a market with a deep HOWO dealer network, or your budget is tightly capped at the USD 55,000 FOB level.
  • Specify both for evaluation if: You are a 50+ unit fleet buyer running mixed duty cycles. A 70/30 split (T5G for flat terrain, X6s for quarry) often delivers the lowest blended TCO.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Weichai WP12 more reliable than the Sinotruk D10?

Both engines have proven reliability records. The Weichai WP12 has a B10 life rating of 1.2 million km versus the D10's 1.0 million km. The WP12 also has a wider global service network through Weichai's independent dealer channels, while the D10 is serviced primarily through Sinotruk dealers. For operators in remote mining regions, the Weichai's broader service network is an advantage.

Can I get the X6s with a Sinotruk D10 engine?

No. The SAGMOTO X6s is offered exclusively with Weichai powertrains. This is a deliberate platform strategy: the X6s is positioned as the Weichai-powered premium platform, while the X3s covers the cost-sensitive segment. Buyers who specifically need a Sinotruk D10 engine should evaluate the HOWO T5G directly.

Conclusion

The SAGMOTO X6s and HOWO T5G are both competent 8x4 dump trucks, but they target different buyer profiles. The X6s is the stronger platform for severe-duty quarry and mining haulage, with a stiffer frame, higher-torque Weichai engine, and Hardox 450 tipper body that delivers longer service life in abrasive applications. The HOWO T5G is the more economical platform for general construction hauling on flat terrain, with lower fuel consumption and a deeper global dealer network. Fleet buyers should evaluate both trucks against their specific duty cycle, terrain, and parts support requirements before committing to an order.
