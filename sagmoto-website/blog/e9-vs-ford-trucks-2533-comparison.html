SAGMOTO E9 vs Ford Trucks 2533: Light-Duty Truck Comparison 2026

September 7, 2026Comparison Guide11 min read

The light-duty distribution segment between 8 and 15 tonnes GVW is increasingly contested by manufacturers from China, Turkey, and India, alongside the traditional European incumbents. Two recent entrants stand out: the SAGMOTO E9 from China and the Ford Trucks 2533 from Turkey. Both are designed for the high-mileage urban and regional distribution duty that African, Middle Eastern and Central Asian fleets demand. Both offer competitive acquisition pricing relative to Mercedes-Benz Atego or Volvo FL. Yet they reflect very different engineering traditions, manufacturing ecosystems, and after-sales support networks.

This guide compares the SAGMOTO E9 and the Ford Trucks 2533 head-to-head across powertrain, payload, chassis, fuel consumption, parts availability, dealer network, and five-year total cost of ownership. The goal is to give procurement managers a transparent evaluation framework for choosing between the two platforms.

Manufacturer Background and Manufacturing Footprint

SAGMOTO is the export brand of Shaanxi Automobile Holding Group (also known as Shacman), a Top-5 Chinese heavy-duty truck manufacturer headquartered in Xi'an, Shaanxi Province. SAGMOTO vehicles are produced at the Xi'an manufacturing complex, which has an annual capacity of 200,000 heavy and medium-duty units. Shaanxi Fenghan Trading is the authorized export distributor, with established dealer networks across 50+ countries.

Ford Trucks is the heavy commercial vehicle division of Ford Otosan, a joint venture between Ford Motor Company and Koç Holding based in Istanbul. Ford Trucks has been producing trucks for the Turkish domestic market since the 1960s and has aggressively expanded into export markets since 2012. The Ford Trucks product family includes the 2533 light-duty, the 4148 medium-duty, and the F-MAX heavy flagship. Manufacturing capacity is approximately 30,000 units annually, with export markets including Europe, the Middle East, Africa, and Central Asia.

Where SAGMOTO benefits from the scale of China's largest commercial vehicle manufacturing base, Ford Trucks benefits from the Turkish engineering tradition, the European brand association through Ford, and the proximity to European and Middle Eastern markets.

Powertrain Architecture

The SAGMOTO E9 light-duty platform is offered with two engine options. The standard Yuchai YC4D130-33 displaces 4.214 litres, producing 130 hp at 2,800 rpm with peak torque of 480 Nm from 1,400 to 1,800 rpm. The optional Cummins ISDe 140-30 engine displaces 4.5 litres, producing 140 hp at 2,500 rpm with peak torque of 540 Nm from 1,400 to 1,800 rpm. Both are inline-four designs with common-rail injection, electronic controls, and Euro 5 emissions compliance as standard, with Euro 6 available for European export markets.

The Ford Trucks 2533 uses the Ford Otosan Ecotorq 4.5-litre inline-four diesel producing 330 hp at 2,200 rpm and 1,200 Nm of peak torque from 1,200 to 1,700 rpm. The Ecotorq is an in-house Ford design and is shared with the larger Ford Trucks models. The engine uses a Bosch common-rail injection system at 2,000 bar pressure, an electronically controlled variable-geometry turbocharger, and SCR after-treatment for Euro 6 compliance. The engine has been validated in Ford's Turkish proving grounds and is supported across the Ford Trucks global network.

The Ford Trucks 2533's significantly higher power output (330 hp vs 130-140 hp) places it in a different segment than the E9. The E9 is a true light-duty truck designed for urban and regional distribution at payloads of 5 to 10 tonnes. The Ford Trucks 2533 is positioned as a higher-GVW distribution truck for 13 to 25 tonnes operations, effectively competing against the SAGMOTO E3 rather than the E9 in many markets.

Buyers running 14-17 tonne payload operations should compare the 2533 against the SAGMOTO E3 or E6, not the E9. Buyers running true light-duty 5-7 tonne payload operations should compare the E9 against the Ford Trucks 1833 or Isuzu Forward, not the 2533.

Direct Comparison: SAGMOTO E9 vs Ford Trucks 1833

For a true apples-to-apples comparison, the SAGMOTO E9 is best benchmarked against the Ford Trucks 1833, which competes directly in the 8-15 tonne GVW segment. The 1833 uses the same Ford Ecotorq 4.5L engine detuned to 180 hp and 700 Nm of torque. It is offered with a manual or automated transmission. The 1833 has a payload of 8-11 tonnes.

With this direct mapping, the SAGMOTO E9 advantage is acquisition cost (USD 35,000-42,000 for the E9 versus USD 60,000-72,000 for the 1833) and parts pricing (Yuchai and Cummins parts are widely available at competitive pricing). The Ford Trucks 1833 advantage is cab quality, NVH refinement, and Turkish-brand association in markets where Ford has strong distribution.

Cab, Comfort and Driver Environment

The SAGMOTO E9 cab is a steel day cab with a 2,080 mm internal height and a width of 2,100 mm. The dashboard places the instrument cluster directly ahead of the driver with a 7-inch central display. Air conditioning is standard. Driver seat suspension is mechanical with optional air suspension. The cab has adequate noise insulation for urban and regional duty.

The Ford Trucks cab is a more refined design, with better interior trim quality, improved NVH, and ergonomic dashboard layout. The cab has air conditioning as standard, air-suspended driver seat as standard, and optional sleeper package for long-distance applications. Ford Trucks have invested in cab design to compete against European premium brands.

For drivers operating in tight labour markets, cab quality is a meaningful consideration for retention. Ford Trucks have a clear advantage in this dimension. For drivers in markets where trucks are operator-driven or owner-driven, the E9's adequate cab quality is generally accepted.

Chassis, Bodywork and Application Flexibility

The SAGMOTO E9 chassis uses 6 mm + 6 mm double-layer steel with a section height of 250 mm and a width of 76 mm. The frame is prepared for body mounting with factory-drilled cross-members and a wide range of rear overhang options from 1,000 mm to 3,500 mm. Common body applications include dry freight boxes, refrigerated bodies, tippers, tankers, and garbage compactors.

The Ford Trucks 2533 chassis uses 8 mm + 8 mm double-layer steel with a section height of 280 mm. The heavier frame supports higher payloads and more demanding body applications including cement bulkers, concrete mixers, and dump bodies. The Ford Trucks frame is also prepared for body mounting with a comprehensive bodybuilder documentation package.

For light-duty distribution with 5-7 tonne payload, the E9 chassis is more than adequate. For higher-payload 14-17 tonne distribution, the Ford Trucks 2533 or SAGMOTO E3 is the correct platform.

Fuel Economy and Operating Costs

In urban distribution at 5-7 tonne payload, the SAGMOTO E9 returns 14 to 18 litres per 100 kilometres. The Cummins ISDe variant achieves 1-2 litres better fuel economy than the Yuchai variant due to its higher torque density and more efficient combustion. Regional distribution at 7-tonne payload with 50% paved and 50% secondary roads raises fuel consumption to 18-22 litres per 100 kilometres.

The Ford Trucks 2533 at 14-17 tonne payload returns 22 to 26 litres per 100 kilometres in equivalent urban distribution and 26-30 litres per 100 kilometres in regional distribution. The Ford's fuel consumption is reasonable for its higher power output and payload capacity.

Scheduled maintenance costs for the E9 are USD 1,200 to 1,800 annually for a typical 60,000-kilometre operating cycle. The Ford Trucks 2533 scheduled maintenance is USD 2,400 to 3,200 for the same cycle due to higher parts prices and Ford authorized workshop rates.

Parts Availability and After-Sales Support

SAGMOTO E9 trucks use Yuchai engines, FAST transmissions, and WABCO braking components. These are widely stocked by aftermarket distributors across Africa, the Middle East, and Central Asia. Shaanxi Fenghan Trading maintains a parts warehouse in Xi'an that ships export orders within 48 hours. Yuchai has over 800 authorized service stations across target markets, and FAST transmissions are supported through Weichai's global dealer network.

Ford Trucks has a growing dealer presence in target countries, but the network density varies. In Morocco, Tunisia, Egypt, and Turkey, the network is mature. In sub-Saharan Africa, the network is concentrated in South Africa, Kenya, Nigeria, and Ghana with mobile service support for surrounding countries. Ford Trucks parts are generally available but at higher prices than Chinese competitors.

Fleet Tip: For fleets operating in markets where parts availability is the dominant risk factor, the SAGMOTO E9's wider service network provides measurable uptime advantage. For fleets operating in markets with established Ford Trucks presence, the Ford brand provides familiarity that supports driver recruitment and workshop skills.

Resale Value and Residual Curves

SAGMOTO E9 trucks retain 50 to 60 percent of their value after five years in target markets. The resale market is supported by the SAGMOTO brand reputation, the durable Yuchai and Cummins engines, and the strong parts availability.

Ford Trucks 2533 retains 55 to 65 percent of its value after five years, supported by the Ford brand recognition, the Turkish engineering reputation, and the strong resale market for European-associated trucks.

Total Cost of Ownership Five-Year Comparison

For a five-year ownership cycle with 60,000 km annual mileage and 5-tonne payload in urban distribution, the SAGMOTO E9 achieves a TCO of USD 0.42 to 0.55 per kilometre. The Ford Trucks 2533 in equivalent cycle at 14-tonne payload achieves USD 0.62 to 0.78 per kilometre. The TCO difference reflects the different payload capacities and acquisition costs.

For a fleet running mixed light-duty and heavier-payload operations, the recommendation is to run a mixed fleet of E9 units for light-duty distribution and 2533 units (or E3 units) for medium-duty distribution. This optimizes capital allocation and operational flexibility.

Decision Framework

Choose the SAGMOTO E9 if your fleet runs true light-duty distribution with 5-7 tonne payloads, prioritizes acquisition cost and parts pricing, operates in markets where Chinese OEMs have established dealer networks, and runs in mixed-route duty cycles where Yuchai and Cummins service support is widely available.

Choose the Ford Trucks 2533 if your fleet runs higher-payload distribution at 14-17 tonnes, values Turkish engineering and brand association, operates in markets with established Ford Trucks presence, and prioritizes cab quality and driver retention.
