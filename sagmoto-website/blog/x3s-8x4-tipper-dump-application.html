SAGMOTO X3s 8x4 Tipper Dump Truck: Application Guide for Aggregate and Mining Haulage 2026

September 7, 2026Industry Application11 min read

The 8×4 tipper dump configuration fills a critical gap in heavy-duty haulage operations between standard 6×4 dumpers and articulated mining trucks. With a payload capacity of 30 to 35 tonnes and a gross vehicle weight of 40 to 49 tonnes, the 8×4 tipper is the workhorse of urban construction aggregate supply, quarry-to-crusher haulage, road construction ballast supply, and short-distance mine-to-stockpile operations. The SAGMOTO X3s 8×4 tipper has been engineered specifically for the duty, with a Weichai WP12.380E22 engine, hub-reduction rear axles, and a reinforced frame designed for severe load cycles.

This application guide examines the SAGMOTO X3s 8×4 tipper configuration across powertrain, chassis, body options, loading compatibility, fuel consumption, and total cost of ownership. The goal is to give fleet procurement managers and operations directors a clear understanding of the operating economics and configuration choices for aggregate and mining haulage applications.

Why 8x4 Tipper Configuration

The 8×4 designation means eight wheels with four powered wheels. The configuration has a single front steering axle, a single rear steering tag axle, and two powered rear axles. This layout provides several advantages over the 6×4 configuration: higher payload capacity (32-35 tonnes versus 25-28 tonnes for 6×4), improved load distribution across multiple axles (reducing road wear and meeting axle-load regulations in markets with strict limits), and improved manoeuvrability for short-radius loading and dumping operations.

The 8×4 configuration has become the standard for aggregate supply in European urban markets with strict axle-load regulations, in Middle Eastern quarry-to-site operations with high throughput requirements, and in African mining operations where short-radius haulage favours articulated manoeuvrability. The trade-off is slightly higher fuel consumption than 6×4 configurations due to the additional weight of the tag axle assembly.

Engineering Insight: The 8×4 tipper configuration delivers approximately 30 percent higher payload per trip than the 6×4 configuration, which directly translates to revenue per trip and per driver shift. For high-utilization fleets, this payload advantage often outweighs the slight fuel consumption penalty.

Powertrain Specifications

The SAGMOTO X3s 8×4 tipper is powered by the Weichai WP12.380E22 inline-six diesel engine displacing 11.596 litres. The engine produces 380 hp at 1,900 rpm and 1,800 Nm of peak torque from 1,200 to 1,600 rpm. The common-rail injection operates at 1,800 bar pressure with electronically controlled injectors. The variable-geometry turbocharger is calibrated for severe-duty cycles including sustained power at low engine speed.

The transmission is the FAST 12JSD180T-B with 12 forward gears including crawler ratios for low-speed manoeuvring at the loader interface. The clutch is a single-plate dry type with hydraulic actuation and 430 mm diameter. The gearbox is paired with a hydraulic retarder for auxiliary braking on loaded descents.

The driveline features two rear axles, each rated at 16 tonnes. Both axles are SAGMOTO-designed hub-reduction units with differential locks and cross-axle locks for soft-ground mobility. The final-drive ratio is 5.263, balancing startability with acceptable cruise rpm at 80 km/h road speed.

Chassis and Frame Engineering

The X3s 8×4 tipper uses a straight-ladder frame constructed from 8 mm + 8 mm double-layer high-strength steel, with a section height of 300 mm and width of 80 mm. The frame is reinforced at the body mounting points with 10 mm internal reinforcement plates. The wheelbase is 1,800 mm + 3,600 mm + 1,400 mm, providing a long stable platform for the body.

Front suspension uses parabolic leaf springs rated at 9 tonnes. The rear suspension uses multi-leaf steel springs rated at 32 tonnes combined. The chassis is prepared for body tipping cylinders with front and rear cross-members rated for 30 tonnes of tipping force. The body is lifted by a single front-mounted telescopic cylinder or optional dual rear-mounted cylinders for heavier bodies.

The chassis is also prepared for tailgate options including air-operated tailgates, clamshell tailgates for asphalt haulage, and rock-style tailgates for aggregate haulage. Body heating systems for asphalt haulage are available with optional exhaust gas recirculation tubes and stone bed protection plates.

Body Options and Application Matching

For aggregate haulage from quarry to crusher or batching plant, the standard steel half-pipe body with a 28 m³ capacity is recommended. The body is constructed from 6 mm high-strength steel with 8 mm reinforcement at the front and tailgate mounting points. The tailgate is a rock-style design with chain-operated release for clean material ejection during tipping.

For coal haulage from mine stockpile to rail loading or thermal power plant, the steel half-pipe body with a 32 m³ capacity and an optional electric tarp cover is recommended. The tarp prevents dust emissions during transit and meets environmental compliance requirements in many markets. The reinforced tailgate with rubber seals is optional for fine coal applications.

For overburden haulage in mining operations, the reinforced steel rock body with 16 mm steel floor and 10 mm sides is recommended. The body is designed for impact loading from mining shovels and front-end loaders. The tailgate is a heavy-duty clamshell design for controlled ejection of large rocks.

For hot-mix asphalt haulage, the insulated aluminium body with stone bed protection is recommended. The body is designed to retain asphalt heat during the journey from batching plant to paving site, extending the working window for placement and compaction. The stone bed protects the floor from impact damage during loading.

Loading Interface and Cycle Time

The SAGMOTO X3s 8×4 tipper is designed for compatibility with all major loader classes from 5-tonne wheel loaders to 35-tonne hydraulic excavators. The body height of 2,800 mm matches the loading height of most mining shovels and large wheel loaders. The body width of 2,500 mm accommodates 5-cubic-metre loader buckets.

Typical cycle time for a quarry-to-crusher aggregate haulage operation is 12 to 18 minutes including loading (3-4 minutes), loaded haul (4-6 minutes), dumping and return (5-8 minutes). For an 8-hour shift with two drivers, the X3s 8×4 tipper can complete 25-35 cycles per shift, delivering 750-1,200 tonnes of aggregate.

For mine-to-stockpile coal haulage, cycle times are slightly longer at 15-25 minutes due to the longer haul distances and the more careful tipping procedure required at the stockpile. Per-shift productivity is 600-900 tonnes.

Fuel Economy and Operating Costs

In aggregate haulage at 32-tonne payload over 5-kilometre average cycle, the X3s 8×4 tipper returns 38 to 46 litres per 100 kilometres. In coal haulage over 8-kilometre average cycle at 30-tonne payload, fuel consumption is 42 to 50 litres per 100 kilometres. These are competitive with comparable Chinese and European 8×4 dump trucks.

Scheduled maintenance costs are USD 3,800 to 4,800 annually for a typical 80,000-kilometre operating cycle. Major components including Weichai engines, FAST transmissions, and SAGMOTO axles are supported by global parts networks, reducing downtime and parts pricing.

Tyre costs are a significant component of operating economics. The X3s 8×4 tipper is typically fitted with 12.00R20 or 315/80R22.5 tyres. A full set of six tyres costs USD 3,500 to 4,500 with a service life of 60,000 to 100,000 kilometres depending on operating surfaces. Rotating tyres between positions extends life by 15-20 percent.

Recommended Configurations by Application

For urban aggregate supply with strict axle-load regulations, specify 12.00R20 tyres on steel wheels, parabolic front springs, a 28 m³ steel half-pipe body, and reinforced rear suspension. The configuration delivers 32-tonne payload with full regulatory compliance.

For quarry-to-crusher haulage on unpaved roads, specify 12.00R20 tyres with reinforced sidewalls, a 32 m³ steel rock body, an air cleaner with pre-cleaner, and reinforced U-bolts. The configuration provides 35-tonne payload capacity with severe-duty durability.

For mining overburden haulage with impact loading, specify a 16 mm steel floor body, 10 mm steel sides, clamshell tailgate, 315/80R22.5 tyres, and optional engine brake. The configuration handles impact loading from mining shovels.

For hot-mix asphalt haulage with thermal retention, specify an insulated aluminium body with stone bed protection, electric tarp cover, and asphalt exhaust heating. The configuration retains asphalt heat for extended working windows.
