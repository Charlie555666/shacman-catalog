Quad Axle Dump Truck OEM: How to Source 8x4 Tippers from China

August 14, 2026 OEM Guide 10 min read

A quad axle dump truck — the 8x4 tipper with two steering axles up front and two driven axles at the rear — is the workhorse of mining, quarrying, and large-scale earthmoving around the world. For fleet owners, construction companies, and importers who want to buy these trucks directly from the factory, China's OEM ecosystem offers the best price-to-capability ratio on the market. But sourcing an 8x4 dump truck OEM is not as simple as picking the cheapest quote: the difference between a truck that survives five years of hard loading and one that cracks its frame in eight months is decided long before the container ship sails. This guide covers what a quad axle dump truck actually is, how Chinese OEM factories build them, how to evaluate a factory, and the complete export process.

What Is a Quad Axle Dump Truck?

A quad axle dump truck has four axles: two steering axles at the front and two driven axles at the rear, configured as 8x4. The two driven rear axles are typically tandem-mounted with inter-axle differentials, and both receive drive torque through a transfer case. The 8x4 layout spreads the loaded weight across eight wheels, which reduces per-tire ground pressure, improves traction on soft or unsealed surfaces, and — critically — keeps the truck within legal axle load limits on public roads while carrying gross combination weights of up to 100 tons.

Compared to a 6x4 tipper, the quad axle platform offers roughly 30% more legal payload capacity in most jurisdictions. Compared to an off-highway rigid dump truck, it offers the decisive advantage of on-road registration: the truck can move itself between sites, return to the yard for maintenance, and handle short haul runs without a lowboy trailer. That dual on/off-road capability is why the 8x4 dominates the mid-range mining and heavy construction segment across Africa, the Middle East, Central Asia, and Latin America.

Why it matters: The quad axle dump truck sits in the sweet spot between a 6x4 construction tipper and a dedicated off-highway mining truck. It hauls 40-60 tons per trip legally, registers for road use, and costs a fraction of a rigid haul truck. This is the platform most large mining contractors buy in fleets.

How Chinese OEM Factories Build Quad Axle Dump Trucks

China's heavy truck OEM industry is dominated by a handful of state-backed groups — Shaanxi Automobile Group (which produces both SHACMAN and SAGMOTO brands), Sinotruk, FAW Jiefang, Dongfeng, and SAIC-Hongyan. These factories share the same underlying supply chain: Cummins and Weichai engines, Fast Gear (Shuanghuan) transmissions, and domestic axle producers. The OEM differentiator is chassis engineering, body quality, and the depth of the export support network.

Chassis and Frame Construction

The frame is the load-bearing skeleton and the first thing to inspect. Premium Chinese 8x4 platforms use a double-layer reinforced frame — a main longitudinal rail with an inner reinforcement liner running the full length of the load section. This construction nearly doubles bending and torsional stiffness compared to a single-rail design, which is essential because dump trucks are loaded by shovels and wheel loaders that impose shock loads far above the static payload rating. Cross-members on mining-spec frames are heavier gauge, and the frame is corrosion-treated for the acidic and alkaline environments found on mine sites.

Driveline Matching

A quad axle dump truck's driveline must be torque-matched to the payload cycle. The two dominant engine choices are:

  • Cummins ISMe 420: 8.9L inline-six, 420 HP @ 2100 RPM, 2000 Nm peak torque between 1100-1700 RPM. Preferred for mining because the torque curve delivers maximum pulling force exactly where a loaded truck climbs out of a pit or ramp.
  • Weichai WP10 / WP12: 9.7-11.6L, 340-430 HP, 1600-2000 Nm. Favored in markets with established Shaanxi service networks; proven in dusty, high-temperature environments with excellent low-end torque.

Both pair with a Fast Gear 12-speed manual (e.g., 12JS240TA rated to 2400 Nm) or an automated manual transmission. The rear axle ratio is selected for the haul profile — a lower ratio for long flat hauls, a higher ratio for steep ramp work.

Dump Body Fabrication

Body steel is where cheap OEMs cut corners and where you should never compromise. Mining-grade bodies use Hardox-class wear-resistant plate with a minimum Brinell hardness of 400 HBW, with floor plates of 8-12mm and side walls of 6-8mm. Standard construction bodies use high-strength steel with optional wear plates. Body volume must be matched to material density — a 30 CBM body filled with iron ore at 2.5 t/m³ carries 75 tons, while the same body with topsoil at 1.2 t/m³ carries only 36 tons. Specify the body for your real material, not a generic maximum.

How to Evaluate a Quad Axle Dump Truck OEM Factory

Before you commit to an OEM, run a structured audit. The five points below separate genuine export-grade factories from trading intermediaries that simply resell whatever chassis they can find.

  1. Manufacturing credentials: Confirm the factory holds a valid vehicle manufacturer license and export qualification. Shaanxi Automobile Group's commercial vehicle division is a full manufacturer, not an assembler of third-party chassis.
  2. Export references: Ask for a list of export destinations with ports, buyers, and volumes over the past two years. Call or email one or two references directly.
  3. Third-party inspection: Budget for a pre-shipment survey by SGS, Bureau Veritas, or a local agent. An OEM that refuses independent inspection is a red flag.
  4. Warranty in writing: Standard is 12-24 months or 100,000-200,000 km on the chassis, with the engine warranted by its original manufacturer (Cummins/Weichai). Get the exact coverage in the contract.
  5. Parts and after-sales: Verify spare parts can be shipped through a stocked export channel. A bonded spare parts program with a warehouse in your region is the strongest signal of a long-term OEM relationship.
Pro Tip: Never buy an 8x4 dump truck on chassis price alone. A USD 5,000 saving on the body steel will cost you USD 15,000 in premature wear-plate replacement within two years. Quote the body spec explicitly in the contract: steel grade, Brinell hardness, floor and wall thickness, and lifting system brand.

The Export Process: From PO to Port

A typical quad axle dump truck export order runs through seven stages: (1) specification confirmation and proforma invoice; (2) contract signing with payment terms (typically 30% deposit / 70% against bill of lading, or TT/LC for larger fleets); (3) production, which takes 20-45 days depending on body customization; (4) third-party pre-shipment inspection; (5) port loading and customs clearance; (6) shipping by RoRo, break-bulk, or container depending on route; (7) delivery, destination clearance, and commissioning support. FOB prices from China for a quad axle dump truck typically range USD 55,000-95,000 depending on engine, body volume, steel grade and options.
