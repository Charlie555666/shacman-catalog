SAGMOTO i9 vs Mercedes-Benz eActros 300: Heavy-Duty Electric Truck Comparison 2026

September 7, 2026Comparison Guide12 min read

European heavy-duty distribution is undergoing its most significant technology shift in a generation. Fleet operators that have run diesel Mercedes-Benz Actros trucks for decades are now evaluating battery-electric alternatives for urban and regional duty. The Mercedes-Benz eActros 300 is the incumbent premium choice, while the SAGMOTO i9 has emerged as a credible challenger with substantially lower acquisition cost and equivalent operational performance for many duty cycles.

This guide compares the SAGMOTO i9 and the Mercedes-Benz eActros 300 across battery technology, range, payload, charging standards, telematics, after-sales support, and five-year total cost of ownership. The goal is to give fleet electrification planners a transparent evaluation framework for choosing between a premium European benchmark and a high-value Chinese challenger.

Platform Background

The SAGMOTO i9 is the heavy-duty battery-electric variant of the SAGMOTO medium and heavy-duty platform family. Launched in 2023 and now in volume production at the SAGMOTO Xi'an facility, the i9 has been deployed in fleet pilots across European, Middle Eastern, Southeast Asian, and Latin American markets. The platform targets urban distribution, refrigerated transport, light-duty container haulage, and parcel logistics.

The Mercedes-Benz eActros 300 is part of the Mercedes-Benz Trucks electromobility programme launched in 2018 and in serial production since 2021. The eActros is built at the Mercedes-Benz Trucks facility in Wörth, Germany, and is sold across European markets and selected export markets. Mercedes-Benz has accumulated the largest fleet validation database for European heavy-duty electric trucks and continues to expand the platform with the eActros 400, 600, and articulated eActros variants.

Where Mercedes-Benz benefits from the European premium brand association and the deepest dealer network for high-voltage service, SAGMOTO benefits from the much larger Chinese EV supply chain and acquisition cost that is 50 to 60 percent lower than the European benchmark.

Battery Technology and Range

The SAGMOTO i9 uses a CATL lithium iron phosphate (LFP) battery pack with a usable capacity of 246 kWh in the standard variant and 350 kWh in the extended range variant. LFP chemistry is favoured for thermal stability, longer cycle life (over 4,000 cycles to 80 percent capacity), and lower cost. The i9's standard range delivers approximately 280 kilometres in mixed urban duty, while the extended range delivers approximately 400 kilometres.

The Mercedes-Benz eActros 300 uses an NMC lithium-ion battery pack with a usable capacity of 336 kWh in the standard three-battery configuration. Mercedes-Benz offers the eActros 400 with 414 kWh in the four-battery configuration and the eActros 600 with over 600 kWh for long-distance haulage. NMC chemistry offers higher energy density but lower cycle life (around 2,500 cycles to 80 percent capacity) compared to LFP.

In the standard comparison between the SAGMOTO i9 standard 246 kWh and the Mercedes-Benz eActros 300 336 kWh, the Mercedes has approximately 35 percent more battery capacity and approximately 30 percent more usable range. In the extended comparison between the SAGMOTO i9 extended 350 kWh and the Mercedes-Benz eActros 300 336 kWh, the SAGMOTO has slightly more energy at lower cost.

Charging Infrastructure Compatibility

The SAGMOTO i9 supports the European CCS2 standard and the Chinese GB/T standard through a dual-port design. Maximum DC charging power is 180 kW for the standard variant and 250 kW for the extended variant. The i9 supports Plug and Charge automatic authentication at compatible charging stations, simplifying operation for fleet drivers.

The Mercedes-Benz eActros 300 supports the CCS2 standard exclusively. Maximum DC charging power is 160 kW for the standard three-battery configuration. Mercedes-Benz has implemented Plug and Charge across its European battery-electric portfolio.

For European fleets operating exclusively on CCS2 infrastructure, both trucks are equally compatible. For fleets operating in China, the Middle East, or Latin America where GB/T infrastructure is also deployed, the SAGMOTO i9's dual-port design provides operational flexibility. For fleets requiring higher charging power for fast turnaround duty cycles, the SAGMOTO i9 extended variant's 250 kW peak is a meaningful differentiator.

Powertrain and Driveline

The SAGMOTO i9 uses a dual-motor configuration with permanent magnet synchronous motors producing a combined 400 kW of peak power and 2,400 Nm of peak torque. The motors drive through a two-speed automatic transmission to a hub-reduction rear axle. The rear axle is rated at 18 tonnes and supports regenerative braking at up to 350 kW of recovery power.

The Mercedes-Benz eActros 300 uses a dual-motor configuration with permanently magnet synchronous motors producing 400 kW of peak power and 970 Nm of continuous torque. The motors drive through a four-speed automatic transmission to a conventional single-reduction rear axle. Regenerative braking recovers up to 280 kW of energy.

The SAGMOTO i9's two-speed transmission and hub-reduction axle provide better low-speed startability for loaded urban duty, while the Mercedes-Benz eActros 300's four-speed transmission provides smoother highway operation. For typical European urban distribution duty, the operational difference is minimal.

Cab, Comfort and Driver Environment

The SAGMOTO i9 cab is the standard SAGMOTO heavy-duty cab with electric-specific instrumentation. The driver display shows battery state of charge, range prediction, energy consumption per kilometre, regenerative braking recovery, and charging schedule. Driver seat is air-suspended as standard. The HVAC system uses a heat pump for improved cold-weather performance.

The Mercedes-Benz eActros 300 uses the standard Mercedes-Benz Actros cab, widely regarded as one of the best truck cabs in the world. The driver display includes the Mercedes-Benz Multimedia Cockpit with comprehensive energy flow visualization, route planning for charging, and pre-heating scheduling. The driver seat is air-suspended as standard with multiple lumbar adjustments. The HVAC system uses a heat pump as standard.

For drivers accustomed to Mercedes-Benz cab quality, the eActros 300 provides the familiar ergonomic layout, NVH refinement, and build quality that supports driver retention. For drivers in emerging markets, the SAGMOTO i9's adequate cab quality is generally well received.

Telematics, Fleet Management and Data Services

The SAGMOTO i9 integrates with the SAGMOTO Fleet platform which provides real-time GPS tracking, battery state of charge, energy consumption analytics, charging schedule optimization, predictive maintenance alerts, and remote diagnostics. API integration with third-party fleet management platforms is supported through REST and MQTT interfaces.

The Mercedes-Benz eActros 300 integrates with the Mercedes-Benz Uptime platform, Fleetboard, and the Mercedes-Benz Truck App Portal. These platforms provide comprehensive battery health monitoring, energy flow analytics, route optimization, charging infrastructure mapping, and integration with major fleet management platforms including SAP, Oracle, and Trimble.

For fleets already standardized on Mercedes-Benz Uptime or Fleetboard, the eActros 300 offers simpler integration. For fleets adopting a platform-agnostic telematics strategy, the SAGMOTO i9's open API architecture provides flexibility.

Total Cost of Ownership Five-Year Comparison

Acquisition cost is the most significant TCO difference. The SAGMOTO i9 is priced at approximately USD 145,000-175,000 for the standard 246 kWh variant, including VAT and standard delivery. The Mercedes-Benz eActros 300 is priced at approximately USD 380,000-450,000 for the equivalent 336 kWh variant. The acquisition cost differential of USD 235,000-275,000 per unit is decisive in the TCO calculation.

Energy cost per kilometre is comparable between the two trucks at approximately USD 0.10-0.16 per kilometre in European electricity markets. Scheduled maintenance is lower for both trucks compared to diesel equivalents. The SAGMOTO i9 has lower scheduled maintenance costs due to simpler driveline and lower parts prices.

For a five-year ownership cycle with 80,000 km annual mileage, the SAGMOTO i9 achieves a TCO of USD 0.42 to 0.55 per kilometre. The Mercedes-Benz eActros 300 achieves USD 0.95 to 1.20 per kilometre in the same duty cycle. The TCO difference is primarily driven by acquisition cost.

Decision Framework

Choose the SAGMOTO i9 if your fleet prioritizes acquisition cost and total cost of ownership, requires operational flexibility across CCS2 and GB/T charging standards, runs high-utilization duty where LFP battery cycle life is decisive, needs competitive EV technology for emerging market deployment, or requires fast turnaround capability with 250 kW DC charging.

Choose the Mercedes-Benz eActros 300 if your fleet prioritizes established European brand association, requires maximum range for longer urban routes, values the Mercedes-Benz Uptime and Fleetboard telematics ecosystem, has standardized fleet management on Mercedes-Benz platforms, or values premium cab quality for driver retention in tight European labour markets.

Many fleets are running pilot deployments of both trucks to gather comparative operational data before scaling. Charging Infrastructure and Grid Considerations

Both trucks require depot charging infrastructure to support operational duty cycles. The SAGMOTO i9's 180 kW standard or 250 kW extended charging capability is within the range of most modern commercial depot chargers. The Mercedes-Benz eActros 300's 160 kW peak benefits from 160 kW or higher depot chargers for fast charging. Fleet operators should plan depot charging infrastructure before truck delivery, including electrical capacity assessment, transformer sizing, and demand charge negotiation with the local utility.

Public charging is increasingly available in Europe through the IONITY, Fastned, Allego, and Tesla Supercharger for Trucks networks. Both trucks can use these networks for opportunity charging during the working day. For overnight depot charging, both trucks integrate with standard AC and DC charging equipment.

Regulatory Compliance and Incentives

Both trucks comply with European Type Approval regulations for fully electric trucks. The SAGMOTO i9 is certified to UN R100 for high-voltage electrical safety, UN R10 for electromagnetic compatibility, and ECE R29 for cab strength. The Mercedes-Benz eActros 300 carries equivalent European certifications and has additional certifications for operation in selected export markets.

Many European countries offer purchase incentives for electric trucks including direct subsidies, tax exemptions, and reduced tolls. Germany's KNO program, France's ADEME, the Netherlands' AanZET, and the UK's OZEV DSR scheme all support commercial EV truck adoption. The incentive value varies between EUR 20,000 and EUR 100,000 per unit depending on country and GVW class.
