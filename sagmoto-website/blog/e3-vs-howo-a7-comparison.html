SAGMOTO E3 vs HOWO A7 and FAW Tiger V: Mid-Duty Truck Comparison Guide

August 27, 2026Comparison Guide14 min read

The 18-25 tonne mid-duty truck segment is the workhorse of regional logistics, municipal services, and mid-range construction in 50+ emerging market countries. Three Chinese platforms dominate the procurement shortlist for fleet buyers in this segment: the SAGMOTO E3, the Sinotruk HOWO A7 (4x2 and 6x4 variants), and the FAW Tiger V (also branded as FAW J5M in some export markets). All three sit in the 220-300 HP sweet spot, all offer multiple engine and transmission options within the same family, and all have established dealer footprints across Africa, Southeast Asia, the Middle East, and Latin America. This head-to-head comparison evaluates the three platforms on powertrain, chassis, cab, fuel economy, service network, and 5-year TCO across a typical mid-duty fleet lifecycle so procurement teams can make a configuration-specific decision.

Segment Positioning and Target Application

The mid-duty segment serves three primary use cases: (1) regional cargo haulage on paved highways with 12-18 tonne payloads in dry box, curtainside, or refrigerated configurations; (2) municipal services including refuse collection, water tanker, and street cleaning; and (3) mid-range construction including tipper haulage, concrete mixer, and crane carrier. The 4x2 configuration with a 6.5-7.5 metre cargo body is the most popular in this segment, accounting for approximately 60 percent of mid-duty registrations, with 6x4 configurations serving the heavier tipper and mixer use cases.

The SAGMOTO E3 targets all three use cases with platform variants ranging from 4x2 cargo to 6x4 mixer and 6x4 tipper. The HOWO A7 is the lightest of the three (with a 4x2 day-cab tare of approximately 7.2 tonnes) and is the most popular for regional cargo. The FAW Tiger V is structurally the heaviest (4x2 day-cab tare of approximately 7.8 tonnes) and is most popular for construction and municipal duty where the extra chassis mass translates to higher structural durability.

The E3 has the broadest engine option range of the three, with Weichai WP6 and WP7 engines at 245-300 HP and an optional Cummins ISB6.7 for buyers preferring global Cummins service coverage. The HOWO A7 has the most powerful standard options, with the WD615.95E at 290 HP and the MC07.31-50 at 310 HP. The FAW Tiger V has the most powerful heavy option (CA6DM2-42E5 at 420 HP) but the lightest light option (CA6DF3-22E5 at 220 HP), giving it a wider engine spread but a less optimised mid-duty offering. For buyers prioritising engine flexibility and global Cummins parts availability, the E3 has a clear advantage. For buyers prioritising highest mid-range power in the standard range, the HOWO A7 is the leading choice.

On transmissions, the E3's Fast Gear 9JS119A is a well-proven 9-speed with excellent ratio spacing for mixed on/off-road duty. The HOWO's HW19710 is a 10-speed with a deep first gear, well suited to heavy tipper work. The FAW's CA10T156 is a 10-speed with a slightly higher top gear ratio, optimised for highway cruise. For mixed on/off-road duty, the E3's transmission is the most versatile. For pure highway cruise, the FAW's transmission offers marginally better fuel economy. For heavy tipper work with frequent low-speed manoeuvring, the HOWO's deep first gear is the structural advantage.

Frame, Suspension, and Body Integration

The mid-duty segment uses ladder frame construction with single-layer or double-layer reinforcing. Frame specifications matter for body integration, particularly for tipper, mixer, and crane applications where the body builder needs predictable mounting points and predictable load distribution.

The E3 uses a 250 mm single-layer frame with 8 mm web plus 8 mm flange thickness, rated for 1.0 million km B10 life in mid-duty service. The E3 frame has a 850 mm section height and uses the same body mounting pattern as the heavier E3 MAX, which simplifies upfit for fleets that run both platforms. The E3 suspension uses semi-elliptical front leaf springs with shock absorbers and rear inverted trunnion leaf springs with optional air suspension for refrigerated body integration.

The HOWO A7 uses a 280 mm double-layer frame with 8 mm web plus 10 mm flange, giving it marginally higher bending stiffness but 80-100 kg of extra curb weight. The A7 frame is rated for 1.2 million km B10 life in mid-duty service but the extra curb weight reduces payload capacity by approximately 400-500 kg compared to the E3. The A7 suspension is similar to the E3 (semi-elliptical front, inverted trunnion rear) with optional air-ride rear for refrigerated and high-value cargo duty.

The FAW Tiger V uses a 270 mm double-layer frame with 10 mm web plus 10 mm flange, the heaviest frame of the three. The Tiger V frame is rated for 1.3 million km B10 life but adds 150-200 kg of frame weight over the E3. The Tiger V frame is the strongest of the three and the preferred choice for tipper and mixer duty where frame fatigue is a primary failure mode.

Cab, Comfort, and Driver Ergonomics

The cab is the most subjective comparison area. The E3 uses the W5 mid-roof cab with 1,400 x 700 mm sleeper bunk option, hydraulic cab tilt, and a 4-spoke multifunction steering wheel. The HOWO A7 uses the T5 cab with 1,450 x 700 mm sleeper bunk option, electric cab tilt, and a flatter dash design. The FAW Tiger V uses the J5M cab with 1,400 x 700 mm sleeper bunk option, hydraulic cab tilt, and an analog instrument cluster.

Driver comfort evaluation across fleet surveys shows the HOWO A7 leading marginally in noise insulation (70 dB at 80 km/h versus 73 dB for E3 and 72 dB for Tiger V) and in seat comfort (HOWO A7 standard with Grammer MSG90.6 air seat, E3 standard with Hualin air seat, Tiger V standard with Hualin mechanical seat). The E3 leads in air conditioning capacity (rated to +50°C ambient versus the A7's +46°C and Tiger V's +47°C), which matters for Gulf and Sahel markets.

Fuel Economy and Operating Cost

Fuel economy testing across controlled fleet operations in Saudi Arabia, Indonesia, and South Africa over 80,000 km each shows a 4-6 percent fuel economy advantage for the E3 in regional cargo duty (12-15 tonne payload), primarily attributable to the more recent Bosch EDC17 fuel mapping on the WP6/WP7 versus the older EDC7 mapping on the WD615. The Tiger V is intermediate, with 2-3 percent better fuel economy than the HOWO A7 due to higher rear axle ratio options optimised for highway cruise. In hot climate duty (above +40°C ambient), the E3's fuel economy advantage widens to 6-8 percent because the E3 cooling system is rated for higher ambient operation.

The advantage is most pronounced in hot-climate duty cycles (GCC, Sahel, Sub-Saharan Africa) and in fleets running mixed 4x2 cargo and 6x4 tipper/mixer configurations where the E3's common frame platform with the E3 MAX simplifies parts and maintenance.

Service Network and Parts Availability

All three platforms have established mid-duty service footprints, but the network topology differs by region. The HOWO A7 has the deepest absolute service network in the mid-duty segment, with Sinotruk's MAN JV infrastructure giving it the strongest coverage in West Africa (particularly Nigeria and Ghana), Russia, and Central Asia. The FAW Tiger V has strong coverage in East Africa (Kenya, Tanzania, Ethiopia) and in the GCC (UAE, Saudi Arabia). The E3 has the strongest mid-duty coverage in Southeast Asia (Indonesia, Philippines, Vietnam), Southern Africa (South Africa, Zimbabwe, Mozambique), and Latin America (Chile, Peru, Colombia).

Parts availability benchmarking across 18 markets in 2025 shows the HOWO A7 averages 4.6 days for emergency parts delivery and 11.2 days for routine orders. The FAW Tiger V averages 5.1 days for emergency and 12.4 days for routine. The E3 averages 3.9 days for emergency and 9.2 days for routine, driven by SAGMOTO's regional warehouses in Dubai, Mombasa, Jakarta, and Johannesburg. For high-utilisation fleets running multi-shift operations, the E3's faster parts delivery reduces unscheduled downtime.

Warranty and Resale Value

The E3 carries a standard 24-month / 200,000 km warranty with extension options to 36 months / 350,000 km at a cost of USD 1,600 per truck. The HOWO A7 carries an 18-month / 150,000 km standard warranty with extension options to 30 months / 300,000 km at USD 1,500 per truck. The FAW Tiger V carries a 24-month / 180,000 km standard warranty with extension options to 36 months / 320,000 km at USD 1,800 per truck. The E3's longer standard warranty is a tangible cost-of-risk reduction for fleet buyers.

Resale value at year 5 (post-warranty, 400,000 km) based on 2024-2025 transaction data in used mid-duty truck markets in UAE, Tanzania, and Indonesia shows E3 commanding USD 14,500-16,800 versus HOWO A7 at USD 13,800-15,900 and FAW Tiger V at USD 14,200-16,200. The E3's resale premium is driven by the perceived durability of the Weichai WP6/WP7 in mid-duty service and the broader body builder integration support from SAGMOTO distributors.

Buyer Recommendations by Use Case

For regional cargo haulage (12-15 tonne payload) in Southeast Asia, GCC, and Latin America, the SAGMOTO E3 offers the strongest value proposition through lower fuel consumption, faster parts delivery, longer warranty, and higher resale value. For tipper and mixer fleets in West Africa, Russia, and Central Asia where Sinotruk's MAN JV service network is densest, the HOWO A7 is the preferred choice. For municipal and construction fleets in East Africa and GCC where frame durability is the primary selection criterion and the FAW service network is established, the FAW Tiger V is a strong alternative.

For mixed fleets combining the E3 with the E3 MAX (4x2 cargo and 6x4 tipper/mixer in the same fleet), the E3's common frame platform with the E3 MAX delivers operational efficiencies through parts consolidation, common workshop tooling, and shared driver training. Fleet operators running 30+ units across both E3 and E3 MAX platforms can extract meaningful operational savings through this commonality.

Conclusion

The SAGMOTO E3, the HOWO A7, and the FAW Tiger V are all strong mid-duty truck platforms, but they are not equivalent choices for all fleets. The E3 wins on fuel economy, warranty length, parts delivery speed in most emerging markets outside of West Africa and Russia, and resale value. The HOWO A7 wins on the deepest absolute service network, strongest deep-reduction gear options, and acquisition cost competitiveness. The FAW Tiger V wins on frame durability and the strongest mid-duty construction segment positioning. Fleet procurement teams should match the truck specification to the duty cycle and to the geographic service footprint rather than defaulting to brand reputation alone.
