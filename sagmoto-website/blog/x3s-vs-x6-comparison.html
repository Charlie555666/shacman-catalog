SAGMOTO X3s vs X6 Dump Truck: Which Heavy-Duty Tipper Fits Your Operation?

August 20, 2026Comparison Guide9 min read

The SAGMOTO X3s and X6 are the two flagship dump truck platforms in the SAGMOTO heavy-duty lineup. Both target construction, quarry, and mining operators, but they serve different payload classes, duty cycles, and operating environments. Choosing between them is one of the most common specification decisions facing fleet buyers evaluating SAGMOTO heavy dump trucks for export markets.

This head-to-head comparison examines the X3s and X6 across engine, transmission, drivetrain, payload, frame strength, fuel economy, and total cost of ownership — and provides a decision framework for fleet buyers.

Quick Summary: X3s vs X6

The X3s is the heavier, more powerful platform designed for 30T to 90T GVW operations with up to 460HP engine options. The X6 is the lighter, more agile platform designed for 25T to 60T GVW operations with up to 420HP engine options. The X3s dominates in deep mining and quarry; the X6 dominates in construction aggregate transport and road-building operations.

Engine Deep Dive

X3s: Weichai and Cummins Power

The X3s is offered with two engine families. The Weichai WP10/WP12 series engines are produced by Weichai Power — China's largest heavy-duty engine manufacturer — and are known for low fuel consumption and accessible parts pricing. The Cummins ISME engines are produced by the Dongfeng-Cummins joint venture and share architecture with Cummins' global heavy-duty platforms.

For buyers exporting to markets with existing Weichai or Cummins service infrastructure, both engine options offer immediate parts compatibility. For buyers prioritizing the lowest acquisition cost, the Weichai option typically saves USD 4,000 to USD 7,000 per unit compared to the equivalent Cummins tier.

X6: Cummins ISD and Yuchai YC6MK Power

The X6 is offered with the Cummins ISD series (more compact, lighter than the ISME) and the Yuchai YC6MK series — a 10L engine designed for medium-heavy duty applications. The YC6MK is widely used in Chinese dump truck platforms and is known for high torque output at low RPM.

For buyers who already run Yuchai-powered equipment in their fleet, the X6 with YC6MK engine provides a unified engine inventory across the fleet — simplifying maintenance training and spare parts management.

Power Selection Guidance: For 6x4 aggregate hauling on flat-to-moderate terrain, the X6 with 380HP engine is fully adequate and saves USD 10,000+ vs the X3s equivalent. For 8x4 quarry operations with steep inclines and heavy rock loads, the X3s with 460HP engine provides necessary power margin.

Drivetrain and Axles

The X3s uses Hande 16T hub-reduction rear axles as standard for quarry configurations — the hub-reduction design provides massive torque multiplication for low-speed, high-load cycles. For highway-speed construction dump operations, the X3s is also available with Hande 13T single-reduction axles.

The X6 uses Hande 13T single-reduction rear axles across all configurations, with optional Hande 16T single-reduction for the heaviest 60T GVW configurations. The single-reduction design is lighter and simpler to maintain than hub-reduction — preferred by operators prioritizing low maintenance cost over maximum low-speed torque.

Both platforms offer differential locks (cross-axle and inter-axle on 6x4 and 8x4) as standard for off-road traction.

Frame and Body Strength

The X3s uses a heavier frame (320mm x 10mm) designed for the higher GVW and tipper body capacity. The X6 uses a lighter frame (280mm x 8mm) suited to its 25-60T GVW envelope. Both frames use high-strength steel (tensile strength 700+ MPa) and a reinforced cross-member pattern.

Tipper body construction differs significantly between the two platforms:

  • X3s: Hardox 450 or NM400 wear-resistant steel with 8mm floor + 6mm sides. Floor reinforcements on 600mm spacing. Rated for 30,000+ load cycles with abrasive rock.
  • X6: NM400 or Q345B with 6mm floor + 5mm sides. Floor reinforcements on 800mm spacing. Rated for 20,000+ load cycles with sand, gravel, and aggregate.

For operators hauling abrasive granite, basalt, or quartzite, the X3s body specification delivers 30-50% longer body life. For operators hauling limestone, sand, or construction debris, the X6 body is fully adequate and significantly lower cost.

Decision Framework: Which to Choose?

Choose the X3s if:

  • GVW requirement exceeds 60T
  • Tipper body capacity needs exceed 25 m³
  • Operating terrain includes steep inclines (10%+ grade)
  • Material hauled is abrasive rock (granite, basalt, quartzite)
  • Operations are 24/7 deep mining or large quarry

Choose the X6 if:

  • GVW requirement is 25-60T
  • Tipper body capacity is 15-25 m³
  • Operating terrain is flat-to-moderate (under 8% grade)
  • Material hauled is construction aggregate, sand, or limestone
  • Operations are urban construction or road-building

Frequently Asked Questions

Can the X3s and X6 share spare parts in a mixed fleet?

Limited parts sharing exists between the X3s and X6 — primarily consumables (filters, belts, brake linings) and certain cab/body components. However, drivetrain components (engine, transmission, axles) are NOT shared between the platforms due to different power classes and load ratings. Fleet buyers operating both platforms should expect separate spare parts inventories for each platform, with limited overlap (typically 15-20% of parts SKUs).

Which platform is easier to maintain in remote locations?

The X6 is generally easier to maintain in remote locations due to its simpler drivetrain architecture (no hub-reduction axles as standard, smaller engine displacement, lighter components). However, both platforms benefit from the same Hande axle and Fast Gear transmission service training. For remote mining sites, the X3s is preferred due to its higher payload and lower per-tonne operating cost. Buyers should also consider that X3s components are heavier and require more substantial workshop lifting equipment for major service operations.

Is the X3s available with the same Cummins engines as the X6?

Partially. The Cummins ISME series engines are available on both platforms, but the X3s uses the larger ISME 420-460HP variants while the X6 uses the smaller ISME 380HP and below. The X3s can also be specified with the Weichai WP10/WP12 engines that are not available on the X6. The X6 alternatively offers Yuchai YC6MK engines that are not available on the X3s.

Conclusion

Both the X3s and X6 are excellent dump truck platforms, but they target different operating envelopes. The X3s is the heavy-duty mining and quarry specialist with the structural strength and engine power for the most demanding applications. The X6 is the agile construction and aggregate specialist with the fuel economy and payload class for the broader market. For fleet buyers running mixed operations, the optimal answer is often to run both — X6 for high-cycle construction work, X3s for deep quarry duty.
