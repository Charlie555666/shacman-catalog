SAGMOTO E3 vs Mercedes-Benz Atego: Mid-Duty Truck Comparison 2026

September 7, 2026Comparison Guide11 min read

The mid-duty distribution segment between 12 and 18 tonnes gross vehicle weight is one of the most competitive corners of the global truck market. Fleet buyers in this segment typically run tight urban and regional routes, balance payload capacity against manoeuvrability, and count every percentage point of fuel economy. Two very different trucks have converged on this segment: the European benchmark Mercedes-Benz Atego and the Chinese challenger SAGMOTO E3. This guide compares them across powertrain, chassis, cab, operating economics, parts support and resale value to give procurement managers a clear framework for choosing between premium European engineering and high-value Chinese manufacturing.

The comparison focuses on the 13-tonne rigid distribution configuration most commonly specified by African, Middle Eastern and Central Asian fleets. Both trucks are sold in left-hand-drive and right-hand-drive variants. Both can be mounted with box bodies, refrigerated bodies, tippers or tankers. Yet their design philosophies are very different: the Atego is engineered for narrow urban streets, premium driver retention, and class-leading residual values, while the SAGMOTO E3 is engineered for high payload, simple mechanical serviceability, and aggressive acquisition cost.

Powertrain Architecture

The SAGMOTO E3 mid-duty platform is powered by a Yuchai YC6MK diesel engine displacing 9.84 litres. The standard variant produces 350 hp at 2,100 rpm with peak torque of 1,550 Nm delivered from 1,200 to 1,600 rpm. The engine uses a Bosch common-rail injection system at 1,800 bar pressure, an electronically controlled variable-geometry turbocharger, and a cooled exhaust gas recirculation loop calibrated to Euro 5 emissions. The engine block is a wet-sleeve design that simplifies field overhaul, and service intervals are 25,000 km for oil and filters.

The Mercedes-Benz Atego 1324 L platform uses the Mercedes OM936 inline-six diesel displacing 7.7 litres. The OM936 produces 238 hp at 2,200 rpm and 850 Nm of torque from 1,200 to 1,600 rpm. Mercedes uses its own XPI common-rail system at 2,400 bar, a two-stage turbocharger, and SCR after-treatment with AdBlue dosing for Euro 6 emissions compliance. Service intervals are extended to 60,000 km but require Mercedes-Benz approved fluids and diagnostic tools.

Where the E3 uses a FAST 9-speed manual or ZF 9S automatic transmission, the Atego is available with the Mercedes G211-9 manual or the PowerShift 8 automated manual. Driveline configurations differ: the E3 uses hub-reduction single rear axles with a 4.63 final-drive ratio, while the Atego uses portal axles with a 3.92 ratio optimized for higher road speed cruising.

The 700 Nm difference is decisive for buyers operating on unpaved roads.

Payload, Bodywork and Chassis Flexibility

The SAGMOTO E3 is offered in two GVW classes: 18 tonnes and 25 tonnes. The 25-tonne variant accommodates a body-and-payload combination of 17 tonnes, which is sufficient for a 7-metre curtain-side body with a 13-tonne payload or a 22 m³ tipper. The frame uses 8 mm + 8 mm double-layer steel with a section height of 280 mm and is prepared for body mounting with a factory cross-member drilling pattern and rear overhang options of 1,100 mm to 2,800 mm.

The Atego 1324 is configured as a 13.5-tonne GVW rigid with a payload of 6 to 8 tonnes after bodywork. Mercedes offers body integration packages for dry freight, refrigerated, tipper, and beverage distribution. The frame is lighter than the E3's at 6 mm + 6 mm but is engineered for shorter bodies in urban duty cycles. Where Mercedes excels is in bodybuilder electronic integration: the Atego provides body wiring harnesses, multiplexed CAN-bus body signals, and PTO programming that can be configured through Mercedes-Benz diagnostic tools.

For African and Middle Eastern fleets running long-distance distribution between cities, the E3's higher payload gives a meaningful revenue advantage. For European fleets running multi-drop urban distribution, the Atego's payload is sufficient and its bodybuilder integration reduces commissioning time.

Cab, Comfort and Driver Retention

The SAGMOTO E3 cab is a steel cab with semi-suspended mounting. The 2,080 mm internal height accommodates most drivers comfortably, and the 2,200 mm wide cab provides room for a passenger seat. Air conditioning is standard, and a sleeper package is available for two-crew operations. The dashboard places the instrument cluster ahead of the driver with a 7-inch central display for trip data and fault code retrieval. Driver seat suspension is mechanical as standard with an air-suspended upgrade option.

The Mercedes-Benz Atego uses the ClassicSpace or BigSpace cab with 1,910 mm or 2,130 mm internal height respectively. Interior trim quality, NVH refinement, and ergonomic design are class-leading. The instrument cluster is configurable through the multi-function steering wheel. The driver's seat is air-suspended as standard with multiple lumbar adjustments. Cab access is excellent due to low step-in height and wide-opening doors.

Driver retention is a key consideration in tight labour markets. Mercedes has a long-standing reputation for cab quality and total driver comfort, which translates into measurable retention rates for fleet operators in Europe. In emerging markets, the Atego's cab advantage is meaningful but the E3's cab is generally regarded as adequate for the duty cycle and climatic conditions where it operates.

Fuel Economy and Operating Costs

In independent testing, the SAGMOTO E3 returns 22 to 26 litres per 100 kilometres in regional distribution at 16 tonnes payload on paved roads. Mixed-route operation with 40 percent unpaved sectors raises consumption to 28-32 litres per 100 kilometres. The YC6MK uses a fuel-only after-treatment system in Euro 5 form, which eliminates the AdBlue logistics burden.

The Mercedes Atego returns 17 to 19 litres per 100 kilometres in equivalent urban and regional duty at lower payloads, partly because of the smaller engine displacement and partly because of Mercedes' BlueTec efficiency calibration. The AdBlue consumption is roughly 4-6 percent of fuel consumption, adding USD 0.50-0.80 per 100 km in AdBlue cost but reducing NOx emissions to Euro 6 levels.

Scheduled maintenance costs differ substantially. The E3's annual scheduled maintenance is USD 1,800 to 2,400 for a typical 80,000-kilometre operating cycle. The Atego's annual scheduled maintenance is USD 3,200 to 4,200 for the same cycle due to higher parts prices and Mercedes-Benz approved workshop rates. Over a five-year ownership period, the E3 saves approximately USD 7,000 to 9,000 in scheduled maintenance costs.

Parts Availability and After-Sales Support

Mercedes-Benz maintains an extensive global dealer network with parts availability typically within 24 to 72 hours in major markets and 5 to 14 days in remote locations. Mercedes parts prices are 2.5 to 4 times higher than equivalent Chinese parts, but quality and traceability are assured through Mercedes-Benz service documentation.

The SAGMOTO E3 uses Yuchai engines, FAST or ZF transmissions, and WABCO braking components. These are widely stocked by aftermarket distributors in Africa, the Middle East and Central Asia. Shaanxi Fenghan Trading maintains a parts warehouse in Xi'an that ships export orders within 48 hours. Yuchai has over 800 authorized service stations across target markets, and FAST transmissions are supported through Weichai's global dealer network. Typical Yuchai parts are 30 to 50 percent the cost of equivalent Mercedes parts.

Fleet Tip: For buyers in markets where Mercedes-Benz service is concentrated in one or two cities, the SAGMOTO E3's parts ecosystem can dramatically reduce downtime. The wider the truck's geographic spread, the more compelling the E3's after-sales story becomes.

Resale Value and Residual Curves

Mercedes-Benz trucks traditionally retain 45 to 55 percent of their original value after five years in developed markets with strong Mercedes-Benz dealer networks. In emerging markets where Mercedes-Benz is a prestige brand, residuals can reach 60 percent or higher. The Atego's residual value is supported by Mercedes-Benz certified pre-owned programmes and a robust secondary market for parts.

SAGMOTO E3 trucks retain 50 to 65 percent of their value after five years in target markets such as Nigeria, Kenya, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Egypt, and Kazakhstan. The resale market is supported by the strong brand recognition of SAGMOTO as the export brand of Shacman, and by the durability demonstrated by early units that have completed 300,000+ kilometre cycles. SAGMOTO's higher residual in many emerging markets reflects the lower initial depreciation and strong parts availability.

Total Cost of Ownership Five-Year Comparison

For a five-year ownership cycle with 80,000 km annual mileage, 16-tonne payload and mixed-route operation, the E3 typically achieves a TCO of USD 0.62 to 0.78 per kilometre. The Atego achieves USD 0.85 to 1.05 per kilometre in the same duty cycle. The E3 advantage comes primarily from acquisition cost differential, scheduled maintenance, and parts pricing. The Atego's advantage is fuel efficiency and resale value in Mercedes-strong markets.

Decision Framework: When to Choose Each Truck

Choose the SAGMOTO E3 if your fleet prioritises payload capacity, operates on mixed paved and unpaved routes, runs in markets where Mercedes-Benz service is distant, and demands low acquisition cost and predictable operating economics. The E3 is also the right answer when the duty cycle involves construction, infrastructure, agriculture or municipal service where high torque is required.

Choose the Mercedes-Benz Atego if your fleet operates in urban European duty cycles, values driver retention through cab quality, requires Euro 6 emissions compliance, and operates in markets where Mercedes-Benz residual value and brand prestige are decisive for end-customer relationships.

Many fleet operators run mixed fleets that include both trucks. The E3 handles heavy regional distribution and infrastructure duty, while the Atego handles high-frequency urban routes.
