SAGMOTO X6 for Heavy Haul and Lowboy Transport: Application Guide 2026

September 2, 2026Industry Application10 min read

Heavy haul and machinery transport is a specialized segment that requires more than just a powerful engine. Operators must consider frame strength, fifth-wheel capacity, axle loading, braking performance, route planning, and permit compliance. The SAGMOTO X6 mid-heavy truck, when configured as a 6x4 tractor with a reinforced chassis and appropriate rear axle ratio, is capable of pulling lowbed and lowboy trailers carrying excavators, bulldozers, cranes, and other construction equipment. This application guide explains how to specify and operate the X6 for heavy haul duty.

Why the X6 Works for Heavy Haul

The X6 sits between the lighter X3s and the heavier X9s in the SAGMOTO range. Its 6x4 tractor configuration offers a practical balance of power, weight, and cost for machinery transport companies that typically haul loads between 30 and 60 tonnes gross combination weight. The X6's Weichai WP10 engine provides sufficient torque for low-speed starts with heavy trailers, while its double-reduction rear axles and reinforced frame handle the concentrated loads imposed by lowboy kingpins.

Compared to premium European tractors, the X6 offers a significantly lower acquisition cost, which is important for small and medium machinery transport companies that may not operate continuously. Compared to older used trucks, the X6 offers new-vehicle reliability, warranty coverage, and modern safety features.

GCW Guidance: The X6 6x4 tractor is rated for a maximum gross combination weight of approximately 55,000 kg to 65,000 kg depending on axle and suspension specification. For loads above this range, operators should consider the X9s or Z3 heavy-haul tractors, which offer higher GCW ratings and larger engines.

Lowboy Trailer Matching

Selecting the right trailer is as important as selecting the tractor. Lowboy trailers for the X6 typically have two or three axles and are designed to keep the load deck close to the ground for safe loading of tall equipment. Common trailer types include:

  • Fixed gooseneck lowboy: Simple and light, suitable for loads up to 40 tonnes GCW.
  • Detachable gooseneck lowboy: Allows equipment to be driven onto the trailer from the front, essential for non-operational machinery.
  • Step-deck lowboy: Combines a low deck section with a higher rear section, useful for mixed loads.
  • Extendable lowboy: Deck length can be extended for long items such as crane booms and wind turbine blades.

The fifth-wheel height, kingpin location, and trailer axle spacing must be matched to the tractor to ensure proper weight distribution. A poorly matched trailer can overload the tractor's rear axle even when the total GCW is within rating.

Operational Safety and Permits

Heavy haul operations require careful route planning. Operators must check bridge weight limits, tunnel clearances, road width, and turning radius at intersections. Many jurisdictions require special permits for loads exceeding standard legal dimensions or weights. The X6's compact cab and good visibility help drivers navigate tight urban and industrial routes.

Braking performance is critical when descending grades with heavy trailers. The X6 is equipped with engine exhaust brakes as standard, and hydraulic retarders are optional. Trailers should have their own braking systems properly connected to the tractor's air supply, and load binders must be inspected at every stop.

Loading Safety: Always load the heaviest part of the machinery over the trailer axles, not behind them or over the tractor fifth wheel. Proper weight distribution prevents trailer sway, reduces tractor rear axle overload, and improves braking stability. Use certified load binders and blocking to prevent movement during transport.

Tire and Rim Specification

Tire selection is critical for heavy haul because tires are the contact point that transfers tractive effort, braking force, and vertical load to the road. For X6 heavy-haul tractors, Fenghan Trading recommends 315/80R22.5 or 12.00R24 radial tires with reinforced load ratings for the drive axles. The front steer axle can use 315/80R22.5 or 295/80R22.5 depending on axle load and local availability.

For mining and quarry operations where puncture risk is high, cut-resistant compounds and wider tread patterns improve durability. In muddy conditions, aggressive lug patterns provide better traction during low-speed loading and unloading. Dual tire configurations on the rear tandem are standard for the X6 6x4 tractor. Operators should maintain tire pressures according to load and check daily for cuts, bulges, and abnormal wear caused by misalignment or overloading.

Maintenance for Heavy Haul

Heavy haul duty is more severe than normal line-haul because of high torque demands, frequent low-speed maneuvering, and heavy braking. Maintenance intervals should be shortened compared to standard highway operation. Engine oil and filters should be changed every 10,000 km or 250 hours. The clutch, transmission, and differential should be inspected for abnormal wear every 20,000 km. Brake linings and drums require regular inspection because heavy loads generate significantly more heat.
