SAGMOTO i9 Electric Refrigerated Truck: Cold Chain Logistics Application Guide

August 25, 2026Industry Application11 min read

The SAGMOTO i9 electric platform has emerged as a serious contender for urban and suburban cold chain logistics, where zero-emission regulations, low-noise night delivery windows, and the high cost of diesel reefer fuel have created strong demand for electric refrigerated trucks. With an integrated LFP battery pack, a permanent magnet drive motor, and a factory-engineered electric reefer integration, the i9 delivers a turnkey zero-emission cold chain solution for fleet operators running supermarket distribution, pharmaceutical transport, and perishable food delivery. This application guide examines how the i9 fits into cold chain operations, the technical integration with refrigeration bodies, the depot charging infrastructure requirements, and the 5-year total cost of ownership compared to diesel reefer alternatives.

Cold Chain Market Drivers for Electric Refrigerated Trucks

Three regulatory and operational drivers are accelerating electric refrigerated truck adoption. First, zero-emission zones (ZEZs) are being implemented in over 320 European cities and 80+ cities in Asia-Pacific, with London, Paris, Madrid, Shenzhen, and Singapore leading the way. Diesel reefer trucks face daily access charges of EUR 15-50 in these zones, making electric alternatives economically compelling. Second, low-noise night delivery windows (typically 22:00 to 06:00) are increasingly mandated for urban food distribution, and electric trucks operate at 20-30 dB lower noise than diesel equivalents. Third, the diesel reefer fuel cost (typically USD 4,500-7,000 per year for a 12 m3 box truck) is eliminated entirely with an electric reefer running off the traction battery.

Operational Insight: The i9's integrated electric reefer draws power from the main traction battery, eliminating the separate diesel reefer engine entirely. This reduces weight by approximately 280 kg, eliminates reefer fuel cost, and removes the most common maintenance item on a refrigerated truck (the diesel reefer engine service). The trade-off is reduced traction range when the reefer is running, typically 12-18 percent depending on setpoint temperature and ambient conditions.

The LFP battery chemistry is a deliberate choice for cold chain applications. LFP has superior thermal stability compared to NMC, with a thermal runaway threshold approximately 200°C higher, which is important when the reefer body is operating at sub-zero temperatures and the battery is cycled deeply. LFP also has higher cycle life (3,000+ cycles to 80 percent capacity) which suits the high daily cycle count of urban cold chain operations.

Electric Reefer Integration

The i9 cold chain variant uses an electric reefer unit that draws power directly from the traction battery via a 400V DC-DC converter. The reefer compressor is electric (no belt drive), and the reefer control system is integrated with the vehicle's telematics, allowing remote temperature monitoring and pre-cooling schedules. The reefer unit is supplied by Carrier, Thermo King, or Guchen (Chinese) depending on buyer preference and destination market.

Three reefer configurations are offered:

  • Single-temperature freezer (-25°C to +5°C): For frozen food and ice cream distribution. Most energy-intensive configuration, reducing traction range by approximately 18 percent.
  • Single-temperature chiller (0°C to +12°C): For fresh produce, dairy, and meat. Moderate energy draw, reducing traction range by approximately 10 percent.
  • Dual-temperature (split box): For mixed frozen and chilled loads, with a movable bulkhead dividing the box into two compartments. The most flexible configuration, reducing traction range by approximately 14 percent.

Depot Charging Infrastructure

The i9 cold chain variant is designed for depot-based overnight charging, with optional DC fast charging for opportunity charging during the day. The recommended depot charging setup is a 22 kW AC charger per vehicle, providing a full 0-100 percent charge in 6 hours. This fits the typical urban cold chain duty cycle where vehicles return to depot between 18:00 and 22:00 and depart between 04:00 and 06:00 the following morning.

For high-utilisation operations running two shifts, a 120 kW DC fast charger is recommended, providing a 20-80 percent charge in 65 minutes. This enables a mid-day opportunity charge during the 60-90 minute driver break window. The depot electrical supply should be sized for the number of simultaneous charges: a 10-vehicle fleet with 22 kW AC chargers requires a 220 kW depot supply (approximately 350 amps at 400V three-phase).

Route Case Study: Supermarket Cold Chain Distribution

A representative SAGMOTO i9 cold chain customer is a supermarket chain running a 30-vehicle refrigerated distribution fleet in a major Asian capital city. The duty cycle is 18-22 delivery stops per route, 180-220 km daily distance, 12-14 hour shift including 3-4 hours of stationary reefer operation at delivery stops. The fleet runs 6 days per week, 50 weeks per year (300 days).

Before transitioning to i9 electric reefers, the fleet used Euro V diesel reefer trucks with an average fuel cost of USD 16,900 per vehicle per year (traction + reefer diesel combined). After transitioning to i9 electric reefers, the energy cost dropped to USD 3,600 per vehicle per year (depot electricity for traction and reefer combined), a USD 13,300 annual saving per vehicle. Across the 30-vehicle fleet, the annual energy saving is USD 399,000, and the ZEZ access charge avoidance adds a further USD 84,000 annual saving. The full fleet transition payback period was 2.8 years.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does cold weather affect the i9 battery range?

In ambient temperatures below 0°C, the LFP battery range reduces by approximately 15-20 percent due to increased internal resistance and cabin heating demand. The i9 includes a battery thermal management system that pre-heats the battery to 15°C before departure, which mitigates most of the cold-weather range loss. For operations in regions with sustained sub-zero temperatures (Northern Europe, Central Asia, Northern China), the high-capacity 150 kWh battery is strongly recommended.

Can the reefer operate while the vehicle is being charged?

Yes. The i9 supports simultaneous charging and reefer operation. During 22 kW AC depot charging, the reefer draws power from the charging input rather than the battery, allowing the battery to charge at full rate while the reefer maintains temperature. This is critical for overnight pre-cooling operations where the reefer must maintain temperature from depot departure through the full delivery route.

What is the battery warranty for the i9 cold chain variant?

The LFP battery is warranted for 8 years or 500,000 km, whichever comes first, with a guaranteed minimum 70 percent capacity retention at the end of the warranty period. This is one of the longest battery warranties in the electric commercial vehicle segment and reflects the proven cycle life of LFP chemistry.
