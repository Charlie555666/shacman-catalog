SAGMOTO E1st Winter Cold-Start Maintenance: Heavy-Duty Service Guide 2026

September 4, 2026Maintenance & After-sales9 min read

The SAGMOTO E1st is the flagship tractor in the SAGMOTO range, designed for long-haul and heavy-haul operations across diverse climates. In regions where winter temperatures drop below freezing for extended periods — including Russia, Kazakhstan, Mongolia, northern China, and parts of Eastern Europe — cold-start reliability becomes a decisive factor in fleet uptime. A single failed start at minus 25 degrees Celsius can delay a scheduled delivery, freeze coolant lines, and damage batteries.

This maintenance guide provides a complete winter cold-start protocol for the E1st. It covers battery care, coolant selection, fuel management, lubrication, air system protection, starting procedures, and daily inspection routines. Following these recommendations will reduce cold-start failures, extend component life, and protect the significant capital investment represented by an E1st tractor fleet.

Battery and Electrical System Care

The E1st uses a 24-volt electrical system with two 12-volt maintenance-free batteries connected in series. Cold weather reduces battery cranking power by approximately 1 percent for every degree Celsius below 20 degrees. At minus 20 degrees, a fully charged battery may deliver only 40-50 percent of its warm-weather cranking amperage. For fleets operating in severe winter conditions, upgrading to cold-cranking-amps (CCA) rated batteries of 850 A or higher is recommended.

Battery heaters or insulated battery boxes should be fitted where overnight temperatures regularly fall below minus 15 degrees. A block heater rated at 1,000 to 2,000 watts should be connected to the engine coolant circuit and plugged in for at least two hours before starting. Engine coolant heaters not only improve startability but also reduce cold-start wear by bringing lubricating oil up to operating temperature faster.

Critical Reminder: Never attempt to start a cold E1st with weak batteries. Repeated cranking overheats the starter motor and can damage the ring gear. Always verify battery voltage is above 24.5 volts before the first start attempt, and use a booster pack or jump-start procedure only as a last resort.

Coolant and Cooling System

The E1st's cooling system should be filled year-round with a 50/50 mixture of ethylene-glycol antifreeze and demineralized water. In extreme cold climates, the antifreeze concentration can be increased to 60 percent, providing freeze protection down to approximately minus 55 degrees Celsius. Do not exceed 67 percent antifreeze concentration, as this reduces heat transfer efficiency and can cause overheating.

Inspect coolant hoses, clamps, and the radiator for leaks before winter begins. A small coolant leak that goes unnoticed in summer can become catastrophic when frozen coolant expands. The pressure cap should be tested and replaced if it cannot maintain the rated system pressure. The coolant level should be checked daily and topped up only with the approved premixed coolant.

Fuel System Winterization

The E1st's Weichai WP13 engine requires clean diesel fuel with appropriate cold-weather properties. Standard diesel fuel begins to cloud and gel at temperatures between 0 and minus 10 degrees Celsius, depending on local refining. Winter diesel with a cloud point at least 6 degrees below the expected minimum temperature must be used. In extreme cold, arctic diesel or kerosene blending may be necessary.

Water in the fuel system is the leading cause of winter fuel-system failures. Drain the water separator every 5,000 kilometres or weekly in cold weather, whichever comes first. Replace the fuel filter at the start of winter and carry a spare filter in the cab. If the truck is parked overnight in severe cold, keep the fuel tank at least half full to reduce condensation.

Lubrication and Greasing

Engine oil viscosity should be reduced in winter. For ambient temperatures down to minus 20 degrees, use 5W-40 or 0W-40 synthetic diesel engine oil meeting API CI-4 or CJ-4 specifications. For temperatures below minus 20 degrees, use 0W-30 or a cold-climate synthetic approved by Weichai. Transmission and axle oils should also be checked for winter-grade ratings.

All chassis grease points should be serviced with lithium-complex grease rated for low-temperature performance. Grease hardens in cold weather and can fail to lubricate pins, bushings, and fifth-wheel plates if the wrong grade is used. The fifth wheel should be cleaned and re-greased weekly in winter to prevent locking and uneven trailer coupling.

Air System and Brake Protection

The E1st's air-brake system is vulnerable to freezing if moisture accumulates in air tanks and lines. Drain all air reservoirs daily — wet tank, dry tank, and auxiliary tanks — to remove condensed water. Replace the air dryer cartridge at the beginning of winter and inspect it again at mid-season. Any sign of oil contamination in the air dryer requires immediate compressor service, as oil can damage dryer desiccant and allow moisture into the system.

Check that the automatic drain valves on air tanks are functioning. Frozen valves trap water in the system. Apply antifreeze-approved air-line treatment if the truck operates in sustained sub-zero conditions. Ensure the parking brake spring chambers are protected from road spray, which can freeze the brake shoes to drums overnight.

Cold-Start Procedure

Before starting, activate the block heater for the recommended duration based on temperature. Turn the ignition on and wait for the glow-plug or intake-air heater indicator to extinguish — typically 10-30 seconds in moderate cold and up to 60 seconds at very low temperatures. Crank the engine for no more than 15 seconds. If the engine does not start, wait 60 seconds before a second attempt to allow the starter to cool.

After starting, allow the engine to idle at 800-1,000 rpm for 3-5 minutes before loading or driving. Do not rev the engine immediately. Monitor oil pressure and coolant temperature before moving. Drive gently for the first 10-15 minutes until normal operating temperatures are reached. Avoid high-load operation until the coolant temperature reaches 70 degrees Celsius.

Tyre and Wheel Care in Cold Climates

Tyre performance degrades significantly in cold weather. Standard highway tyres lose flexibility below 7 degrees Celsius, reducing grip and increasing rolling resistance. For sustained winter operation, fit winter or all-season tyres with the M+S symbol and, where regulations require, the three-peak mountain snowflake (3PMSF) marking. In Russia, Kazakhstan, and Mongolia, studded tyres may be permitted and are highly effective on ice.

Check tyre pressure daily when the tyres are cold. For every 10 degrees Celsius the temperature drops, tyres lose approximately 1 PSI of pressure. Under-inflation increases fuel consumption and tyre wear, while over-inflation reduces grip on snow. Wheel bearings should be inspected for water intrusion before winter, as frozen moisture can cause premature failure. Ensure that tyre chains are carried and that drivers are trained in their installation.

Emergency Equipment and Winter Survival Kit

Every E1st operating in remote cold regions should carry a winter survival kit. The kit should include a shovel, tow strap, sand or kitty litter for traction, high-visibility vest, first-aid kit, thermal blankets, flashlight, spare batteries, portable jump starter, spare coolant, engine oil, fuel additives, water, and non-perishable food. Trucks should also carry a fire extinguisher, warning triangle, and spare fuses.

For routes where mobile phone coverage is unreliable, install a satellite communicator or two-way radio. Drivers should be trained on cold-weather survival basics, including how to stay warm if stranded and when to remain with the vehicle rather than walking for help.

Extended Parking and Storage Procedures

If an E1st must be parked for more than 48 hours in sub-zero temperatures, take additional precautions. Keep the fuel tank full to minimize condensation. Disconnect the batteries or connect a trickle charger to maintain charge. If available, keep the block heater connected to maintain engine temperature. Cover the radiator grille partially to reduce cold airflow, but do not fully block it when the engine is running. Start the engine every 24 hours and let it idle for 15 minutes to circulate fluids and charge the batteries.

Exhaust and After-Treatment Protection

Cold weather affects diesel exhaust after-treatment systems. The E1st's SCR and DPF components require minimum exhaust temperatures to function correctly. Short trips in winter may prevent the DPF from reaching regeneration temperature, leading to soot accumulation and fault codes. Operators should plan at least one extended drive per week at highway speed to allow passive regeneration. If the DPF warning light illuminates, follow the recommended active regeneration procedure before the filter becomes overloaded.

DEF (diesel exhaust fluid) has a freezing point of minus 11 degrees Celsius. The E1st DEF tank is heated by engine coolant, but frozen DEF in supply lines or storage containers can cause dosing failures. Store DEF indoors or in insulated containers, and allow it to thaw naturally if frozen. Never add antifreeze or water to DEF.

Documentation and Record Keeping

Maintain detailed winter service records for each E1st. Log battery voltage, block-heater usage, coolant test results, fuel additives, filter changes, and any cold-start failures. This data helps identify patterns and supports warranty claims. Common Winter Mistakes to Avoid

One of the most common winter mistakes is using summer diesel in freezing temperatures. Summer diesel will gel and clog filters, leaving the truck immobile. Another mistake is neglecting to plug in the block heater because the previous day started easily. Battery condition changes rapidly with temperature, and a marginal battery that starts at minus 10 degrees may fail at minus 20 degrees. A third mistake is allowing the cooling system to freeze due to weak antifreeze. Frozen coolant expands with enough force to crack cylinder heads and radiators, causing catastrophic engine damage.

Finally, some operators use ether starting aids excessively. Ether can ignite too aggressively in a cold diesel engine and cause piston or ring damage. If ether must be used, follow the engine manufacturer's guidelines strictly and never use it with an intake heater or glow plugs active. The safest approach is proper pre-heating, good batteries, and winter-grade fuel.

Summary Winter Maintenance Schedule

Adopt the following schedule for E1st fleets in cold climates: daily — battery voltage, tyre pressure, lights, coolant level, air-tank draining, and fuel-water separator check; weekly — battery electrolyte level, tyre inspection, fifth-wheel lubrication, and cab heater function; monthly — coolant concentration test, alternator output, starter motor draw, block-heater function, and air-dryer cartridge inspection; seasonally — oil and filter change to winter grade, DEF system check, brake adjustment, and underbody wash to remove road salt and chemicals.

Operator Comfort and Cab Heating

Driver comfort affects safety and productivity in winter. The E1st cab heater should be inspected before cold weather arrives, including the heater core, blower motor, and coolant valves. Blocked heater cores reduce cabin temperature and demisting performance. Engine coolant must reach operating temperature for the heater to function effectively, reinforcing the importance of block-heater use. Drivers should carry blankets and warm clothing as backup, especially on long routes where breakdown could leave them stranded in sub-zero conditions.

Summary: Winter-Ready E1st Fleet Management

Winter operation of the SAGMOTO E1st demands attention to batteries, coolant, fuel, lubrication, air systems, and driver procedures. By implementing a structured winter maintenance program, fleet managers can prevent cold-start failures, reduce component wear, and protect drivers in harsh conditions. The investment in block heaters, winter fluids, and training pays dividends in uptime, safety, and vehicle longevity across cold-climate markets.
