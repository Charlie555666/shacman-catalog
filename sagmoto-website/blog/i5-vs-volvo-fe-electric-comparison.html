SAGMOTO i5 vs Volvo FE Electric: Electric Distribution Truck Comparison 2026

September 7, 2026Comparison Guide12 min read

Electric distribution trucks between 12 and 19 tonnes GVW are transforming urban logistics across Europe, the Middle East, and increasingly Asia. Fleet operators committed to decarbonization now have multiple credible options, and the comparison between the SAGMOTO i5 and the Volvo FE Electric is one of the most relevant in this segment. Both trucks are designed for zero-emission urban distribution with payload flexibility and modern charging compatibility. Both are based on proven platforms with extensive validation. Yet they reflect different engineering philosophies, supply chain strategies, and acquisition economics.

This guide compares the SAGMOTO i5 and the Volvo FE Electric head-to-head across battery technology, range, payload, charging standards, telematics, after-sales support, and total cost of ownership. The goal is to give fleet electrification planners a clear framework for choosing between the two platforms.

Platform Background

The SAGMOTO i5 is the dedicated battery-electric variant of the SAGMOTO light-duty platform, manufactured at the SAGMOTO Xi'an facility. The i5 was launched in 2023 and has been deployed in fleet pilots across multiple Chinese cities and export markets in Europe, the Middle East, Southeast Asia, and Latin America. The platform targets urban delivery, refrigerated distribution, light-duty parcel logistics, and waste collection applications.

The Volvo FE Electric is part of the Volvo Trucks global electromobility programme launched in 2019. The FE Electric is built at the Volvo Trucks facility in Blainville, France, and is sold across European markets and selected global export markets. Volvo Trucks has the longest track record of any European manufacturer in serial-produced electric trucks and has accumulated more than 100 million kilometres of customer validation data.

Where Volvo benefits from the longest EV truck track record and the broadest European dealer training, SAGMOTO benefits from the much larger Chinese EV supply chain and significantly more competitive acquisition pricing.

Battery Technology and Range

The SAGMOTO i5 uses a CATL lithium iron phosphate (LFP) battery pack with a usable capacity of 132 kWh in the standard range variant and 198 kWh in the extended range variant. LFP chemistry is favoured for its thermal stability, longer cycle life (over 4,000 cycles to 80 percent capacity), and lower cost compared to NMC chemistry. The i5's standard range delivers approximately 220 kilometres of operation in mixed urban duty, while the extended range delivers approximately 330 kilometres.

The Volvo FE Electric uses a NMC lithium-ion battery pack with a usable capacity of 200 kWh in the standard configuration and 400 kWh in the extended configuration. NMC chemistry offers higher energy density but lower cycle life (around 2,500 cycles to 80 percent capacity) compared to LFP. The Volvo FE delivers approximately 200 kilometres with the 200 kWh battery and approximately 350 kilometres with the 400 kWh battery in mixed urban duty.

In terms of usable range, the two platforms are roughly comparable. In terms of total cost of battery ownership over a 10-year cycle, the SAGMOTO i5's longer cycle life and lower battery replacement cost provide a meaningful advantage, particularly for high-utilization fleets.

Charging Infrastructure Compatibility

The SAGMOTO i5 supports both the European CCS2 standard and the Chinese GB/T standard through a dual-port design. This makes the i5 suitable for fleet buyers operating across both standards, simplifying export operations. The maximum DC charging power is 120 kW, allowing 20-80 percent charging in approximately 60 minutes with the standard battery.

The Volvo FE Electric supports the CCS2 standard exclusively. The maximum DC charging power is 150 kW, allowing 20-80 percent charging in approximately 80 minutes with the standard battery. Volvo Trucks participates in the CharIN consortium and has actively contributed to CCS2 standardization.

For European fleets operating exclusively on CCS2 infrastructure, both trucks are equally compatible. For fleets operating in China, the Middle East, or Latin America where GB/T infrastructure is also deployed, the i5's dual-port design provides operational flexibility. For fleets requiring higher charging power for fast turnaround duty cycles, the Volvo FE Electric has a slight advantage.

Powertrain and Driveline

The SAGMOTO i5 uses a single rear-axle-mounted permanent magnet synchronous motor producing 250 kW of peak power and 1,200 Nm of peak torque. The motor drives through a single-speed transmission to a hub-reduction rear axle. The rear axle is rated at 13 tonnes and is optimized for the lower-speed urban duty cycle of an electric truck. Regenerative braking recovers up to 70 percent of deceleration energy, extending operational range and reducing brake wear.

The Volvo FE Electric uses a dual-motor configuration with a combined peak output of 400 kW and 850 Nm of continuous torque. The motors drive through a two-speed transmission to a single rear axle. The Volvo FE Electric offers multiple regenerative braking modes including a one-pedal driving mode that maximizes energy recovery in urban stop-and-go traffic.

In real-world urban duty, the SAGMOTO i5's simpler single-speed, single-motor configuration delivers comparable operational performance at lower cost and with fewer serviceable components. The Volvo FE Electric's dual-motor configuration provides higher peak power for demanding applications and more sophisticated regenerative braking tuning.

Cab, Comfort and Driver Environment

The SAGMOTO i5 cab is the standard SAGMOTO light-duty cab with electric-specific instrumentation. The 7-inch central display shows battery state of charge, range prediction, energy consumption per kilometre, regenerative braking recovery, and charging schedule. Driver seat is mechanical as standard and air-suspended as optional. Air conditioning is standard with a heat pump system that improves cold-weather range.

The Volvo FE Electric cab is the standard Volvo cab with electric-specific instrumentation. The driver display provides real-time energy flow visualization, route planning for charging, and pre-heating scheduling. The driver seat is air-suspended as standard. The HVAC system uses a heat pump as standard for improved cold-weather performance.

For European drivers accustomed to Volvo cab quality, the FE Electric provides the familiar ergonomic layout, NVH refinement, and build quality that supports driver retention. For drivers in emerging markets, the i5's adequate cab quality is generally well received.

Telematics, Fleet Management and Data Services

Both trucks offer comprehensive telematics integration. The SAGMOTO i5 integrates with the SAGMOTO Fleet platform which provides real-time GPS tracking, battery state of charge, energy consumption analytics, charging schedule optimization, and predictive maintenance alerts. API integration with third-party fleet management platforms is supported through REST and MQTT interfaces.

The Volvo FE Electric integrates with the Volvo Connect platform, one of the most mature commercial vehicle telematics platforms in the industry. Volvo Connect provides battery health diagnostics, route optimization, charging infrastructure mapping, and integration with fleet management platforms including SAP, Oracle, and Trimble.

For fleets already standardized on Volvo Connect or SAP, the Volvo FE Electric offers simpler integration. For fleets adopting a platform-agnostic telematics strategy, the i5's open API architecture provides flexibility.

Total Cost of Ownership Five-Year Comparison

Acquisition cost is the most significant TCO difference. The SAGMOTO i5 is priced at approximately USD 95,000-115,000 for the standard 132 kWh variant, including VAT and standard delivery. The Volvo FE Electric is priced at approximately USD 280,000-330,000 for the equivalent 200 kWh variant. The acquisition cost differential of USD 185,000-215,000 per unit is decisive in the TCO calculation.

Energy cost per kilometre is comparable between the two trucks at approximately USD 0.06-0.09 per kilometre in European electricity markets. Scheduled maintenance is lower for both trucks compared to diesel equivalents. The i5 has lower scheduled maintenance costs due to simpler driveline and lower parts prices.

For a five-year ownership cycle with 60,000 km annual mileage, the SAGMOTO i5 achieves a TCO of USD 0.32 to 0.42 per kilometre. The Volvo FE Electric achieves USD 0.78 to 0.95 per kilometre in the same duty cycle. The TCO difference is primarily driven by acquisition cost.

Decision Framework

Choose the SAGMOTO i5 if your fleet prioritizes acquisition cost and total cost of ownership, requires operational flexibility across CCS2 and GB/T charging standards, runs high-utilization duty where LFP battery cycle life is decisive, or needs competitive EV technology for emerging market deployment.

Choose the Volvo FE Electric if your fleet prioritizes established European brand association, requires maximum range for longer urban routes, values the Volvo Connect telematics ecosystem, or has standardized fleet management on Volvo platforms.

Many fleets are running pilot deployments of both trucks to gather comparative operational data before scaling. Shaanxi Fenghan Trading supports pilot programs with extended warranty, on-site commissioning, and operator training. For fleets evaluating electrification at scale, a side-by-side pilot provides the empirical data needed for procurement decisions.

Charging Infrastructure and Grid Considerations

Both trucks require depot charging infrastructure to support operational duty cycles. The SAGMOTO i5's 120 kW peak is within the capability of most commercial depot chargers from 60 kW upward. The Volvo FE Electric's 150 kW peak benefits from 150 kW or higher depot chargers for fast charging. Fleet operators should plan depot charging infrastructure before truck delivery, including electrical capacity assessment, transformer sizing, and demand charge negotiation with the local utility.

Public charging is increasingly available in Europe through the IONITY, Fastned, Allego, and Tesla Supercharger for Trucks networks. Both the SAGMOTO i5 and the Volvo FE Electric can use these networks for opportunity charging during the working day. For overnight depot charging, both trucks integrate with standard AC and DC charging equipment.

Regulatory Compliance and Incentives

Both trucks comply with European Type Approval regulations for fully electric trucks. The SAGMOTO i5 is certified to UN R100 for high-voltage electrical safety, UN R10 for electromagnetic compatibility, and ECE R29 for cab strength. The Volvo FE Electric carries equivalent European certifications.

Many European countries offer purchase incentives for electric trucks including direct subsidies, tax exemptions, and reduced tolls. Germany's KNO program, France's ADEME, the Netherlands' AanZET, and the UK's OZEV DSR scheme all support commercial EV truck adoption. The incentive value varies between EUR 20,000 and EUR 80,000 per unit depending on country and GVW class.
